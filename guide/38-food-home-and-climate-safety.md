# 38 Food home and climate safety

A few targeted checks can reduce everyday hazards without turning your home into a full-time project.

## 38.1 Use a thermometer and refrigerate perishable food promptly

Keep raw meat separate from ready-to-eat food, clean hands and preparation surfaces, and use a food thermometer with the recommended temperature for the food. Refrigerate perishables within two hours, or one hour when exposed to temperatures above 90°F. Thaw safely in the refrigerator, cold water, or microwave; cook immediately after thawing in cold water or the microwave. Appearance and smell cannot reliably establish safety, and a bargain is not worth eating recalled food.

- **Cost and effort:** A food thermometer has a modest upfront cost; safe handling adds attention.
- **Payoff:** Reduces avoidable food poisoning and wasted meals.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC food poisoning prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/prevention/index.html); [FDA safe food handling](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/safe-food-handling)

## 38.2 Test for radon instead of guessing from the neighborhood

Use EPA guidance to arrange a radon test appropriate to your home and follow the kit instructions. A neighbor’s result does not measure your home. If the result calls for action, obtain qualified mitigation advice and retest after work. Renters can ask the owner about existing results and responsibilities. For a purchase, clarify testing and mitigation in the inspection process before closing.

- **Cost and effort:** Test kits or professional testing cost money; mitigation costs vary.
- **Payoff:** Identifies an invisible household exposure that can be reduced.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance + Practice. [EPA radon testing and reduction resources](https://www.epa.gov/radon)

## 38.3 Check your water source and lead risk before buying a filter

Ask your water utility about service lines and testing, especially in older housing. If lead is a concern, follow local sampling guidance and select a filter certified for lead reduction; replace cartridges on schedule. Use cold water for drinking and cooking. Boiling does not remove lead. A general annual water report may not describe the plumbing inside your home, and private wells need their own testing plan.

- **Cost and effort:** Utility information may be free; tests and filters vary in price.
- **Payoff:** Targets the actual contaminant instead of buying an unsuitable filter.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [EPA lead in drinking water](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/basic-information-about-lead-drinking-water)

## 38.4 Arrange cooling before a heat wave

Check local heat risk and identify a reliably cool place, including a public cooling center if needed. Move demanding outdoor activity to cooler hours, take breaks, and check on people who live alone. Ask your clinician about a heat plan if you have medical conditions or take medicines that affect heat tolerance. Plan for refrigerated medicines and powered equipment during outages; do not change prescriptions on your own.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning can be free; cooling and transportation may cost money.
- **Payoff:** Makes dangerous heat easier to manage before symptoms develop.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC heat and health planning](https://www.cdc.gov/heat-health/about/index.html)

## 38.5 Prepare a cleaner indoor room for wildfire smoke

Check AirNow and local instructions before outdoor activity during smoke events. Identify an indoor room where air can be kept cleaner and consider appropriately sized filtration. Reduce indoor smoke and particle sources. People with heart or lung disease, children, and older adults may need extra precautions. A cleaner room does not override an evacuation order, and a hot sealed room creates another hazard if cooling is unavailable.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning is free; filtration, replacement filters, and cooling have costs.
- **Payoff:** Reduces smoke exposure when sheltering indoors is appropriate.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [AirNow preparation for wildfire smoke](https://www.airnow.gov/wildfires/be-smoke-ready/)
