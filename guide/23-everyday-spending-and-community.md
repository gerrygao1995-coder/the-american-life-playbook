# 23 Everyday spending and community

Make ordinary weeks easier to afford and easier to repeat. Convenience, relationships, and accessibility belong in the calculation.

## 23.1 Plan a few meals you will actually cook

Choose two or three realistic meals, check what is already at home, and make a shopping list around overlapping ingredients. Compare fresh, frozen, and canned options that fit your needs and storage. Build in a quick fallback meal for exhausted evenings. Buying bulk only helps if you can store and use it; include preparation time and transport rather than judging groceries by sticker price alone.

- **Cost and effort:** A short weekly planning session; groceries remain a household expense.
- **Payoff:** Can reduce unused food and unplanned takeout without requiring an elaborate diet.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance + Practice. [USDA nutrition on a budget](https://www.nutrition.gov/topics/food-security-and-access/nutrition-budget)

## 23.2 Assess energy use before financing a major upgrade

Review bills across seasons and ask your utility about an energy assessment. Start with the largest practical sources of waste identified for your home. Compare installed cost, maintenance, expected savings, and how long you will stay before buying equipment. Renters should check permission before changes. Confirm current incentives with the administering program; an old webpage or salesperson’s projection is not an approved rebate.

- **Cost and effort:** Initial bill review is free; assessment and repair prices vary.
- **Payoff:** Prioritizes improvements that fit your actual home and budget.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance + Practice. [Department of Energy home assessment guide](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/guide_to_home_energy_assessments.pdf)

## 23.3 Check the library before adding another subscription

Ask your local public library about digital books, audiobooks, research databases, computer access, learning resources, and community events. Check residency and borrowing rules and try the service before paying for something similar. Collections and waiting lists vary. A library card can also be an entry point to local information and human help when an online form or job search gets confusing.

- **Cost and effort:** Library access is often free for eligible residents; some services have fees.
- **Payoff:** Makes useful learning and entertainment available at low cost.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance + Practice. [USAGov public libraries and available resources](https://www.usa.gov/libraries-and-archives)

## 23.4 Use unit prices and actual usage to compare a deal

Compare equal units, such as price per ounce, and then ask how much you will use before it spoils or becomes clutter. Include membership charges, delivery fees, and the travel required to get a discount. Try a smaller package if you are unsure you like the product. A lower unit price is useful only when the purchase fits your cash flow and real needs.

- **Cost and effort:** Free comparison; a few minutes per unfamiliar purchase is an estimate.
- **Payoff:** Can reduce waste and avoid spending more merely to obtain a discount.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance + Practice. [USDA budget resources including unit pricing](https://www.nutrition.gov/topics/food-security-and-access/nutrition-budget)

## 23.5 Put affordable social time on the calendar

Choose a recurring way to see or contact people: a walk, library group, shared meal, community class, or a call with a friend. Make the first commitment small enough to keep. Reach out for help and offer it where you can without exhausting yourself. If cost, illness, transport, or disability is a barrier, look for an accessible alternative rather than treating isolation as a personal failure.

- **Cost and effort:** Can be free; set a time commitment you can sustain.
- **Payoff:** Builds connection and practical support into ordinary weeks.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC suggestions for improving social connection](https://www.cdc.gov/social-connectedness/improving/index.html)
