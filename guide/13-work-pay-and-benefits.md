# 13. Work, Pay, and Benefits

A better job is a combination of dependable pay, usable benefits, manageable hours, and enforceable rights. Keep your own records and check the rules that apply to your employer and state.

## 13.1 Check your pay against the hours you actually work

Keep a personal record of starting times, finishing times, unpaid breaks, and work performed outside scheduled shifts. Compare it with each pay stub. Covered, nonexempt employees generally qualify for federal overtime after 40 hours in a workweek; exemptions depend on the actual work and applicable requirements. A salary or managerial title alone should not end your inquiry. Check state protections and ask the Wage and Hour Division about unexplained differences.

- **Cost and effort:** Free; estimate five minutes each workday and 15 minutes per paycheck.
- **Payoff:** Creates a usable record for correcting underpayment; recovery depends on the facts and deadlines.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Rule and Practice. [USAGov overtime pay](https://www.usa.gov/overtime-pay); [DOL exemption guidance](https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/whd/flsa/screen75.asp).

## 13.2 Ask about protected leave before using up your options

When illness or caregiving affects work, ask HR for eligibility, notice requirements, certification forms, and the leave policy in writing. Federal FMLA provides eligible employees of covered employers with unpaid, job-protected leave for qualifying reasons, generally up to 12 workweeks. Eligibility includes service, hours, and worksite tests; special rules exist. Ask separately about paid sick time, disability benefits, and state family-leave programs, because job protection and wage replacement are different questions.

- **Cost and effort:** Free to inquire; estimate an hour for initial paperwork, plus possible medical certification costs.
- **Payoff:** Helps preserve employment and coverage while you plan how to replace missed income.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Practice. [USAGov FMLA benefits and eligibility](https://www.usa.gov/family-leave-act).

## 13.3 Report injuries and unsafe conditions promptly

Get needed medical care, tell the provider when an injury happened at work, and follow your employer's reporting process promptly. Keep dates, witnesses, and copies of reports. Ask the relevant workers' compensation program about claim and appeal deadlines; most private-sector and state or local government claims use state systems. OSHA handles workplace safety complaints, not your compensation claim. If you experience retaliation, contact the relevant agency immediately because some deadlines are very short.

- **Cost and effort:** Reporting is generally free; care, eligibility, and payment arrangements depend on the applicable program.
- **Payoff:** Preserves evidence for benefits and gives safety agencies information they can act on.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [Workers' compensation](https://www.usa.gov/workers-compensation); [OSHA worker protections](https://www.osha.gov/workers).

## 13.4 Request accommodations and track discrimination deadlines

If a disability creates a workplace barrier, explain the job-related limitation and ask for a reasonable accommodation through the employer's process. Keep a dated record of requests and responses. ADA accommodation duties apply to covered employers and qualified individuals, with limits such as undue hardship. For suspected discrimination, check EEOC deadlines promptly. An internal grievance generally does not stop the filing clock, and federal employees use a different process.

- **Cost and effort:** Free initial inquiry; estimate 30–60 minutes to organize records, with possible documentation follow-up.
- **Payoff:** May make work accessible and preserve the ability to pursue a complaint.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [EEOC reasonable accommodations](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/enforcement-guidance-reasonable-accommodation-and-undue-hardship-under-ada); [EEOC filing deadlines](https://www.eeoc.gov/time-limits-filing-charge).

## 13.5 Compare offers using a full compensation worksheet

For each offer, list guaranteed cash, expected hours, health premiums, deductible exposure, retirement match and vesting, paid time off, commute, and childcare changes. Put uncertain bonuses and equity in a separate column. Ask for benefit summaries and written clarification of waiting periods. For an ERISA-covered retirement plan, the Summary Plan Description explains eligibility and benefit rules. Your comparison is a planning estimate; actual benefits depend on the governing plan and your circumstances.

- **Cost and effort:** Free; estimate one hour per offer and time for HR follow-up.
- **Payoff:** Reveals whether higher headline pay actually improves your household budget and available time.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Practice and Rule. [IRS guide to Summary Plan Descriptions](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/401k-resource-guide-plan-participants-summary-plan-description).
