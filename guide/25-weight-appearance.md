# 25. Safer Weight Management and Appearance Choices

Choose goals that serve your health and your own preferences. Evaluate the ongoing cost, medical risk, and reversibility of a product or procedure before buying it.

## 25.1 Choose a weight-management plan you can maintain

If weight loss is appropriate for your health, choose a plan with realistic goals, ordinary foods, activity you can sustain, and support for maintaining changes. Ask how progress is measured and what happens after the initial program ends. Avoid promises of rapid loss without changing habits or programs that hide required food and supplement purchases. If eating or weight concerns are becoming distressing or restrictive, discuss them with a qualified clinician.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 30 minutes to compare the full program, including recurring charges; a useful plan need not require branded products.
- **Payoff:** Helps you avoid expensive short-lived plans and focus on changes that fit your life.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [NIDDK: Choosing a safe weight-loss program](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/choosing-a-safe-successful-weight-loss-program).

## 25.2 Discuss obesity treatment as medical care

If weight-related health problems persist, ask a clinician whether structured treatment, prescription medication, or specialist care fits your situation. Adult medication eligibility often considers BMI of at least 30, or at least 27 with a weight-related condition, but eligibility alone does not establish suitability. Discuss side effects, other medicines, pregnancy plans, follow-up, long-term affordability, and what happens if treatment stops. Obtain prescribed medicine through an appropriate pharmacy and follow the actual prescription.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: one dedicated visit plus follow-up; treatment costs and insurance requirements vary substantially.
- **Payoff:** Lets you assess benefits and risks of medical options without treating a prescription as an appearance shortcut.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [NIDDK: Prescription weight-management medicines](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/prescription-medications-treat-overweight-obesity).

## 25.3 Make sun protection your default skin-care habit

Use shade and protective clothing when outdoors and apply a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher to exposed skin. Reapply according to the label, including after swimming or sweating. Avoid tanning beds. Pick a sunscreen you can comfortably use rather than an expensive routine you abandon. Ask a clinician about a changing or concerning skin spot; cosmetic creams cannot determine whether a lesion is harmless.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: a few minutes to prepare for outdoor time; prices vary, and clothing or shade can reduce exposed area.
- **Payoff:** Reduces ultraviolet exposure and helps prevent sun damage without needing a complicated product routine.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [American Academy of Dermatology: Prevent skin cancer](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/skin-cancer/how-to-prevent).

## 25.4 Treat cosmetic injections as a medical procedure

Before fillers or similar procedures, verify the professional's license, relevant training, exact product, approved use, complication plan, and follow-up arrangements. Ask what improvement is realistic and whether removal would be possible. Do not buy injectable fillers for self-injection or use a needle-free filler device at home. Severe pain, vision changes, or unusual skin discoloration during or after a filler procedure need immediate medical attention. Price alone cannot establish safety.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: one consultation before committing; include repeat treatments and potential complication care in the budget.
- **Payoff:** Helps you make an informed appearance decision and recognize complications that should not wait.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [FDA: Dermal fillers](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/aesthetic-cosmetic-devices/dermal-fillers-soft-tissue-fillers).

## 25.5 Understand the lifetime commitment of cosmetic dentistry

Before veneers, ask a licensed dentist which teeth need treatment, how much enamel would be removed, and what alternatives could achieve your goal. Veneers can require irreversible tooth preparation and eventual repair or replacement. Request an itemized plan and ask how gum disease, tooth grinding, and existing decay affect suitability. Take time for an independent opinion before extensive elective work. Keep routine prevention in place regardless of whether you choose a cosmetic procedure.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: one or two consultations; ask about future maintenance costs and confirm coverage, which may exclude cosmetic work.
- **Payoff:** Makes the long-term dental consequences visible before committing healthy teeth and substantial money.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [American Dental Association: Veneers](https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/veneers).
