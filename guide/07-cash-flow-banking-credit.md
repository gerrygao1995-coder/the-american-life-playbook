# 07. Cash Flow, Banking, and Credit

Build a money system that still works during a busy week. Start with the timing of bills, a small buffer, and accounts you understand. The estimates below describe setup effort, not guaranteed savings.

## 07.1 Put paydays and bills on one calendar

List your take-home pay dates, bill due dates, and the balance you need before each payment. Review several months of statements so annual insurance, school expenses, and car repairs do not disappear from the plan. Budget from actual spending first, then choose one adjustment. With variable income, plan around money already received or reliably expected. Ask billers whether a different due date would fit your pay cycle.

- **Cost and effort:** Free; estimate 45–60 minutes initially and 10 minutes each week. A notebook or calendar is enough; paid budgeting software is optional.
- **Payoff:** Makes upcoming cash shortages visible while there is still time to change a payment arrangement or expense.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [CFPB: Assess your spending](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/prepare/assess-your-spending/), [CFPB: Build an emergency fund](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/).

## 07.2 Build a buffer around your actual risks

Choose a first savings target tied to a realistic disruption: an urgent repair, an insurance deductible, or a missed paycheck. Keep it accessible and separate from everyday spending. Transfer an affordable amount after payday, with a low-balance alert to prevent an overdraft. Increase the target as circumstances allow; a household with unstable income may need more runway. Verify deposit coverage instead of assuming money stored in a payment app is insured.

- **Cost and effort:** No required starting amount; estimate 20–30 minutes to set up. The money remains yours, but setting it aside reduces spending room today.
- **Payoff:** Provides options when an unexpected expense arrives and can reduce the need for emergency borrowing.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [CFPB: Build an emergency fund](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/), [CFPB: Deposit insurance and payment apps](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/issue-spotlight-analysis-of-deposit-insurance-coverage-on-funds-stored-through-payment-apps/full-report/).

## 07.3 Make your checking account fit your habits

Compare monthly fees, minimum-balance requirements, ATM access, and deposit availability using your actual banking habits. Read the fee schedule before switching. Review whether you opted into ATM and one-time debit overdraft coverage; opting out does not stop every possible overdraft charge, including those associated with checks or certain electronic payments. Turn on balance alerts and track pending bills. Keep enough in the old account until direct deposits and recurring payments move successfully.

- **Cost and effort:** Comparing accounts is free; estimate one hour plus follow-up across a pay cycle. Opening requirements and account charges vary by institution.
- **Payoff:** Helps avoid preventable fees and payment failures; an advertised interest rate alone does not show an account's value.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Rule; Practice. [CFPB: Compare checking fees and terms](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/should-i-get-a-checking-account-that-pays-interest-en-925/), [CFPB: Know your overdraft options](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/bank-accounts/know-your-overdraft-options/).

## 07.4 Check your reports and freeze unused credit access

Get your credit reports through AnnualCreditReport.com, reached from the FTC's official instructions. Check unfamiliar accounts, balances, and late payments, and dispute errors with the reporting company and information provider. Consider placing a free security freeze separately at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion when you are not applying for credit. Save account access securely and temporarily lift the relevant freeze when needed. A freeze helps restrict new accounts; continue monitoring existing accounts for fraud.

- **Cost and effort:** Reports through the official service and credit freezes are free; estimate 30–60 minutes initially, with extra time for disputes.
- **Payoff:** Helps you spot reporting errors and adds a barrier against someone opening credit in your name.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Rule; Practice. [FTC: Free credit reports](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/free-credit-reports), [FTC: Credit freezes and fraud alerts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/credit-freezes-and-fraud-alerts).

## 07.5 Use credit only when repayment is manageable

If a credit card fits your spending habits, keep purchases within money you can repay and pay the statement balance by its due date. Check your agreement for grace-period rules, especially if you already carry a balance. Set reminders or affordable automatic payments, then verify they clear. Carrying interest-bearing debt is not necessary to build credit. Avoid opening several accounts for discounts, and compare deposits and fees before using a secured card.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate 20 minutes to review settings and a brief monthly statement check. Card fees, deposits, and interest depend on the product and your repayment.
- **Payoff:** Builds a record of timely payments while limiting avoidable financing costs; no particular score increase is promised.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [CFPB: Rebuild your credit](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/credit-reports-and-scores/how-to-rebuild-your-credit/).
