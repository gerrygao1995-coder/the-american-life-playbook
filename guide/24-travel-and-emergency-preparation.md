# 24 Travel and emergency preparation

Prepare for the disruptions your household is likely to face. Put the plan somewhere you can reach without a working phone.

## 24.1 Start emergency supplies with drinking water and a household plan

Store at least one gallon of water per person per day for three days; add more for heat, illness, pregnancy, and pets, and build toward a longer supply if feasible. Keep essential medicines, contact information, chargers, and an evacuation plan accessible. Coordinate assistance for mobility or powered medical equipment. Follow local evacuation instructions; supplies are useful, but they do not make an unsafe location safe.

- **Cost and effort:** Start with what you have; storage space and supplies have costs.
- **Payoff:** Makes short disruptions easier to manage and departure less chaotic.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance + Practice. [CDC emergency water storage](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/how-to-create-and-store-an-emergency-water-supply.html); [CDC all-hazards planning guide, pp. 10–18](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/138803/cdc_138803_DS1.pdf)

## 24.2 Install working carbon monoxide alarms and keep generators outside

Install CO alarms on every level and outside sleeping areas, with battery backup as appropriate, and follow testing instructions. Never run a generator inside a home or garage, even with doors open; CPSC advises placing it outside at least 20 feet from the home with exhaust directed away. If an alarm sounds or poisoning is suspected, get outside to fresh air and call 911.

- **Cost and effort:** Alarms and batteries have an upfront cost; testing takes minutes.
- **Payoff:** Reduces the risk of a fatal hazard you cannot smell or see.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CPSC carbon monoxide prevention](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Carbon-Monoxide-Information-Center)

## 24.3 Check entry rules and medical coverage before a foreign trip

Confirm passport validity, destination entry rules, relevant travel advice, and whether your current insurance pays for care abroad. If a coverage gap matters, compare travel medical and evacuation insurance including exclusions and pre-existing-condition provisions. Keep accessible copies of documents and a trusted contact’s details. U.S. consular assistance is valuable, but the government does not pay your routine medical or unexpected travel bills.

- **Cost and effort:** Research is free; documents and insurance may cost money.
- **Payoff:** Prevents foreseeable travel disruption and exposes expensive coverage gaps.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [State Department international travel checklist](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/checklist.html)

## 24.4 Make every car trip sober, buckled, and free of phone handling

Buckle up in every seating position, set navigation before departure, and keep the phone out of your hands. Arrange a sober driver or another ride before using substances that impair driving. If tiredness makes you unsafe, stop and rest rather than relying on willpower. A short, familiar drive still requires attention; use a suitable child restraint for children and follow its instructions.

- **Cost and effort:** Usually no extra cost; an alternative ride may cost money.
- **Payoff:** Reduces avoidable crash and injury risks.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [NHTSA seat belt guidance](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/seat-belts); [NHTSA distracted driving prevention](https://www.nhtsa.gov/risky-driving/distracted-driving); [NHTSA drowsy driving](https://www.nhtsa.gov/risky-driving/drowsy-driving); [NHTSA drug-impaired driving](https://www.nhtsa.gov/risky-driving/drug-impaired-driving)

## 24.5 Store firearms so an unauthorized person cannot use them

If firearms are present, store them unloaded and locked, with ammunition secured separately, and keep keys or combinations inaccessible to unauthorized people. Hiding a firearm is not secure storage. Ask about storage where children visit. If someone in the home may harm themselves or others, seek immediate crisis help and arrange safer access restrictions consistent with local law; do not attempt to seize a weapon from someone threatening violence.

- **Cost and effort:** Locks or safes have costs; some local programs provide locks.
- **Payoff:** Reduces unauthorized access, including by children and people in crisis.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC firearm injury prevention and secure storage](https://www.cdc.gov/firearm-violence/prevention/index.html)
