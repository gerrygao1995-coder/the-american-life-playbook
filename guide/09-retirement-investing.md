# 09. Retirement and Long-Term Investing

Investing starts with a purpose, a time horizon, and room in your budget. Account tax rules and investment choices are separate decisions. None of these steps guarantees returns or makes market losses impossible.

## 09.1 Read the employer match before setting contributions

Get your workplace plan's Summary Plan Description and identify eligibility, the matching formula, contribution deadlines, fees, and vesting schedule. If cash flow permits, consider contributing enough to receive the available match. Confirm the actual payroll deductions and investment election afterward. Your own contributions are generally yours, but employer contributions may vest over time. Before changing jobs or changing contributions sharply, ask the administrator how the plan treats your situation; employer plans do not all work alike.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate 30–60 minutes to read and confirm settings. Contributions reduce available take-home pay and generally face retirement-account withdrawal restrictions.
- **Payoff:** Helps you receive a benefit you may already be eligible for and understand which employer contributions you can keep.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Rule; Guidance. [Department of Labor: Understand your retirement plan](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/your-retirement-benefits.pdf).

## 09.2 Compare broad funds by holdings and fees

For long-term money, compare diversified funds available in your plan or brokerage. Read the prospectus for holdings, expense ratios, sales charges, and other account costs. A broad index fund can be a useful candidate, but the word “index” does not guarantee low fees or broad diversification. A target-date fund can manage allocation changes, yet funds with the same date can carry different risks. Choose a combination you understand and avoid accidentally duplicating concentrated exposures.

- **Cost and effort:** Research is free; estimate one hour initially. Fund expenses and account charges are ongoing, and investment values can fall.
- **Payoff:** Helps you select understandable exposure and keep more of any returns after fees; a cheaper fund is not automatically suitable.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [Investor.gov: Index funds](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/mutual-funds-and-exchange-traded-4), [Investor.gov: Target-date funds](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/target-date-funds-investor-bulletin).

## 09.3 Match investment risk to when you need the money

Write down each goal, its deadline, and how much loss you could tolerate without abandoning it. Money for an upcoming bill or near-term purchase generally calls for a different approach from retirement decades away. Choose an allocation across assets that fits both your ability and willingness to take risk. Review it periodically or after major life changes. Diversification can reduce concentration risk, but it cannot promise a profit or prevent losses when markets decline.

- **Cost and effort:** Free planning; estimate 30–45 minutes initially and a periodic review. Trading, taxes, and advice may involve costs depending on your accounts.
- **Payoff:** Reduces the chance that a market downturn forces you to sell investments when you need cash for a fixed deadline.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [Investor.gov: Asset allocation and diversification](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/asset-allocation).

## 09.4 Check IRA tax treatment before contributing or rolling over

Compare traditional and Roth IRA eligibility, current-year contribution limits, deduction rules, and withdrawal treatment. Workplace coverage and income can affect the choice; opening an IRA does not itself invest the money. Before moving an old employer account, compare its fees, investments, and protections with the destination. If you choose a rollover, ask both institutions about a direct transfer. Moving pretax money into a Roth account may create taxable income; get qualified help for complex balances.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate one to two hours to compare and initiate. Transfers, investments, and tax advice may carry charges; confirm the tax effect before authorizing a conversion.
- **Payoff:** Helps preserve retirement savings and avoid preventable withholding, rollover mistakes, or unintended tax bills.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule; Guidance. [IRS: Traditional and Roth IRAs](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/traditional-and-roth-iras), [IRS: Retirement-plan and IRA rollovers](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/rollovers-of-retirement-plan-and-ira-distributions).

## 09.5 Confirm HSA eligibility before treating it as savings

Ask your insurer or benefits administrator whether your coverage supports HSA contributions under current rules. Check other coverage, Medicare enrollment, dependent status, and the applicable annual limit, including employer contributions. Eligibility expanded in 2026, so older high-deductible-plan checklists can be incomplete. Keep receipts for qualified medical expenses and enough accessible cash for upcoming care. Invest HSA money only if the account allows it and your medical budget can tolerate market losses and account fees.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate 30–60 minutes to verify eligibility and fees, then retain records. Contributions use current cash; state tax treatment may differ from federal treatment.
- **Payoff:** Uses an available medical tax benefit correctly while reducing the risk of excess contributions or needing to sell investments for care.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule; Practice. [IRS: HSA eligibility and qualified expenses](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969), [IRS: Expanded HSA eligibility guidance](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-provide-guidance-on-new-tax-benefits-for-health-savings-account-participants-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill).
