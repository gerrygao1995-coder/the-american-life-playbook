# 21 Aging care and end of life

Separate health coverage, retirement income, caregiving, and legal authority. They involve different decisions and deadlines.

## 21.1 Put Medicare enrollment on the calendar before age 65

Most people first become eligible around 65; the usual initial window spans seven months around that birthday month. Confirm your exact dates with Medicare. Ask how employer size and current employment affect enrollment; COBRA and retiree coverage are not interchangeable with coverage from current employment. If you have an HSA, ask about retroactive Part A coverage and when both you and your employer must stop contributions before applying.

- **Cost and effort:** Free eligibility check; premiums and coverage costs vary.
- **Payoff:** Helps prevent avoidable gaps and late-enrollment penalties.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule. [Medicare enrollment timing and existing coverage](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/when-can-i-sign-up-for-medicare); [Medicare and working past 65](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/medicare-basics/working-past-65)

## 21.2 Compare Social Security claiming dates using your own record

Review your earnings record and estimated retirement benefits in your Social Security account. Eligible workers can generally claim from 62, with a higher monthly benefit for delaying up to 70. Consider health, household income needs, work, and spouse or survivor benefits before choosing. The best date is personal; a headline about the largest monthly payment does not decide the household’s best plan.

- **Cost and effort:** Free account review; allow time for a household income comparison.
- **Payoff:** Turns a lasting income decision into a comparison using your own numbers.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule + Practice. [SSA retirement planning](https://www.ssa.gov/retirement/plan-for-retirement)

## 21.3 Budget for everyday care separately from Medicare

List the help a person needs with bathing, meals, dressing, medication, transportation, and supervision. Medicare generally does not pay for ongoing custodial long-term care. Ask about local aging services, Medicaid eligibility, home-based support, and respite options before a caregiver burns out. A temporary skilled-care benefit is not a promise to pay for indefinite assisted living or help at home.

- **Cost and effort:** Needs assessment takes time; private care costs can be substantial.
- **Payoff:** Makes the care funding gap visible while there is time to plan.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule + Practice. [Medicare long-term care coverage limits](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/long-term-care)

## 21.4 Choose decision makers while you can express your wishes

Discuss medical preferences with a trusted person and complete advance-care documents that meet your state’s requirements. Give accessible copies to that person and your clinicians. Separately arrange valid authority for financial decisions; a health care proxy and a financial power of attorney serve different purposes. Review wills and beneficiary designations with appropriate help after major family changes rather than assuming one document handles everything.

- **Cost and effort:** Some forms are free; legal review and witnessing costs vary.
- **Payoff:** Makes wishes and authority clearer during illness or incapacity.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance + Practice. [NIH advance-care planning checklist](https://magazine.medlineplus.gov/article/advance-care-plan-a-checklist-for-the-future/); [CFPB managing someone else’s money](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/managing-someone-elses-money/)

## 21.5 Ask for itemized funeral prices before choosing a package

Ask funeral providers for prices and compare the specific services your family wants. Covered providers must give you a General Price List when you inquire in person about arrangements, and provide required price information over the phone. Ask which charges are optional and why any item is required. Bring a trusted person to help take notes; a more expensive package is not a measure of love.

- **Cost and effort:** Comparing quotes is free; final arrangements vary widely.
- **Payoff:** Preserves choice during a difficult and time-pressured purchase.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule + Practice. [FTC Funeral Rule and itemized price requirements](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-funeral-rule)
