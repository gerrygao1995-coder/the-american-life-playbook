# 31. Buy household work and services with clear terms

Most comparison shopping works better when the scope stays constant. Record what you are buying, who is responsible, what changes the price, and how the agreement ends.

## 31.1 Verify the contractor and permit requirements yourself

Check your state or local licensing authority for the contractor's current status and appropriate trade classification where licensing applies. Verify insurance with the insurer or agent, and ask the building department which permits and inspections your project requires. Obtain comparable written bids and check recent references. A business card saying “licensed and insured” is not verification, and a general business license may not authorize the trade you need.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: one to two hours for initial verification and references; inspections, permits, specialist assessments, and project quotations may have separate charges.
- **Payoff:** You can evaluate who will do the work and which legal or insurance requirements still need attention before paying.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance / Practice. California's checklist illustrates the process; use your own jurisdiction's requirements. [CSLB contractor verification guide](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/Finding_The_Right_Contractor.aspx).

## 31.2 Put the scope and changes in writing

Before work begins, specify materials, model numbers, preparation, cleanup, schedule, payment milestones, warranties, and who obtains permits. For changes, record the added or removed work, price, and timing before authorizing it. Ask your local licensing or consumer agency about deposit limits, cancellation rights, and lien protections. The linked California rules are an example of why location matters; their thresholds and requirements are not nationwide rules.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: one to two hours to review a straightforward contract; substantial renovations or unclear terms may warrant paid legal or technical review.
- **Payoff:** Both parties have a common record of the promised work and the effect of later changes.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Practice / Rule. Legal provisions in the linked source apply to California. [CSLB guide to home improvement contracts](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/Home_Improvement_Contracts/What_Is_A_Contract.aspx).

## 31.3 Evaluate solar as a long-term contract

Compare purchase, loan, lease, and power-purchase offers using the same expected production and utility assumptions. Read escalators, financing charges, roof work, maintenance duties, and home-sale or early-exit terms. Verify interconnection and billing arrangements with your utility. Confirm every proposed incentive with the administering agency for your project and completion date; do not build affordability around a salesperson's tax-credit promise or an outdated online percentage.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: several hours to collect competing offers and review a roof and energy assessment; professional advice may be useful for financing or tax questions.
- **Payoff:** You can distinguish electricity savings assumptions from obligations that remain even if your circumstances change.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance / Practice. Incentives and utility programs vary and can change. [FTC solar buying guide](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/solar-power-your-home), [DOE current savings programs](https://www.energy.gov/save).

## 31.4 Compare internet and mobile service by the whole bill

List your actual speed, data, coverage, and reliability needs before comparing offers. Request the price after promotions, equipment rental, activation, taxes, fees, device installments, and cancellation obligations. Read the available broadband label alongside the full agreement. Check service at your exact address and usual destinations; an advertised coverage area is not a performance promise. Save the offer and inspect the first bill for mismatches.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: 45–90 minutes to compare plans; switching may require equipment returns, installation, device payoff, or a temporary overlap in service.
- **Payoff:** You can compare the service you need at its continuing cost and document a billing dispute if one arises.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Practice / Guidance. The FCC map uses provider-reported availability. [FCC broadband map guide](https://help.bdc.fcc.gov/hc/en-us/articles/10467446103579-How-to-Use-the-FCC-s-National-Broadband-Map), [Fairfax County explanation of broadband labels](https://www.fairfaxcounty.gov/cableconsumer/cable/frequently-asked-questions).

## 31.5 Compare repair and replacement over useful service

Get a diagnosis and written repair estimate before replacing an expensive appliance. Compare the repair's expected reliability with replacement purchase, delivery, installation, disposal, and operating costs. Use the EnergyGuide information and your own utility rates when comparing appliances; an efficient model still costs money to buy. Check warranty coverage, parts availability, and fit. Treat safety defects and required professional work separately from a simple price comparison.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: 30–60 minutes of comparison plus a service visit; diagnostic fees and specialist advice vary by equipment and location.
- **Payoff:** You can make the decision around usable service and total expense, instead of a universal repair-percentage rule.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Practice / Guidance. Energy estimates depend on equipment and use; repair reliability remains uncertain. [DOE appliance purchase and operating costs](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2021-08/ES-KitchenAppliances_080221.pdf).
