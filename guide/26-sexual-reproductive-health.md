# 26. Sexual Health and Reproductive Choices

Practical protection works best with informed, voluntary decisions. Use timely clinical care for exposures, symptoms, and pregnancy questions, and check the rules where care will take place.

## 26.1 Match STI prevention and testing to your actual exposures

Use condoms or other appropriate barriers correctly and consistently, recognizing that protection is incomplete for infections spread through uncovered skin. Discuss partners, sexual practices, symptoms, and relevant exposures honestly with a clinician so testing covers the right infections and body sites. A urine test does not automatically cover throat or rectal infections. Ask when to test and whether repeat testing is needed after the particular exposure.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 15 minutes to locate a clinic and discuss costs; free or reduced-cost testing depends on local programs.
- **Payoff:** Makes prevention and testing more relevant than ordering an unspecified 'full panel' and assuming everything is covered.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC: Condom use](https://www.cdc.gov/condom-use/index.html). [CDC: Getting tested for STIs](https://www.cdc.gov/sti/testing/).

## 26.2 Act immediately after a possible HIV exposure

After a possible HIV exposure, contact an emergency department, urgent care, or a clinician immediately to ask about post-exposure prophylaxis, or PEP. It must start within 72 hours, and earlier is better; do not wait for symptoms or a partner's test result before seeking evaluation. If exposures may recur, ask about pre-exposure prophylaxis, or PrEP. PrEP needs appropriate testing and follow-up and does not prevent other sexually transmitted infections.

- **Cost and effort:** Seek evaluation promptly; medicine, visit charges, and assistance programs vary. Planning estimate: save a local care option in advance.
- **Payoff:** Helps you use time-sensitive HIV prevention and consider an ongoing prevention plan when appropriate.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC: HIV post-exposure prophylaxis](https://www.cdc.gov/stophivtogether/hiv-prevention/pep.html). [CDC: Preventing HIV with PrEP](https://www.cdc.gov/hiv/prevention/prep.html).

## 26.3 Choose contraception around your preferences and health

Compare methods by effectiveness, side effects, medical suitability, cost, privacy, reversibility, and how much daily attention you want to give them. Ask what starting, stopping, or removing the method involves. Your preference matters, including the choice not to use a method. Most contraceptives do not protect against STIs. After unprotected sex or a method failure, ask promptly about emergency contraception because options and effectiveness depend on timing and circumstances.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 20–30 minutes to review options; visit, prescription, procedure, and removal costs depend on coverage.
- **Payoff:** Helps you select a method you can use comfortably and know what to do if it fails.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC: Contraception and birth-control methods](https://www.cdc.gov/contraception/about/index.html).

## 26.4 Make consent explicit and stop when it is absent

Check that everyone freely agrees to the sexual activity and can make that choice. A relationship, previous sex, clothing, silence, or pressure does not establish consent. Respect a change of mind and stop if someone cannot or does not agree. After an assault, prioritize immediate safety and seek medical care promptly for injuries, pregnancy prevention, and infection-related care. A sexual-assault support service can explain local options without making the decision for you.

- **Cost and effort:** A consent conversation costs nothing. After assault, medical, advocacy, and reporting options differ locally; ask about confidential support.
- **Payoff:** Supports bodily autonomy and helps people access timely care and support after harm.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [Office on Women's Health: Sexual assault](https://womenshealth.gov/relationships-and-safety/sexual-assault-and-rape/sexual-assault).

## 26.5 Get timely, noncoercive help with pregnancy options

After a positive pregnancy test, contact a qualified health professional to discuss your health, pregnancy timing, and options: parenting, adoption after continuing the pregnancy, or abortion. Ask which services the provider actually offers or can refer for, what costs apply, and how soon a decision affects available care. Abortion access and legal requirements vary by state and can change; verify current rules where care will occur rather than relying on an old map.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 30 minutes to identify qualified care and request an appointment; clinical costs and travel needs vary.
- **Payoff:** Provides information while choices are time-sensitive and keeps your preferences central to the decision.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [American Academy of Family Physicians: Unexpected pregnancy](https://familydoctor.org/what-to-do-when-your-pregnancy-is-unexpected/).
