# 14. Job Loss and Financial Assistance

After an income shock, act first on applications, coverage, and deadlines. Assistance programs have separate eligibility rules; being turned down for one does not settle your eligibility for another.

## 14.1 Apply for unemployment through the correct state

Use the official unemployment agency for the state where you worked; ask for filing guidance if you worked remotely or across state lines. Apply promptly after job loss and let the agency decide eligibility. Wage history, separation circumstances, work availability, and ongoing requirements vary by state. Save confirmation numbers, follow certification instructions, report earnings as required, and read every decision. If denied, use the appeal deadline in the notice instead of assuming the decision is final.

- **Cost and effort:** Free application; estimate one to two hours initially, then recurring certifications and job-search records.
- **Payoff:** May replace part of lost wages while you look for work; payment is not guaranteed.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Practice. [USAGov unemployment benefits](https://www.usa.gov/unemployment-benefits).

## 14.2 Compare health coverage before the enrollment window closes

Write down when employer coverage actually ends, then compare COBRA, a household member's employer plan, Marketplace coverage, and Medicaid eligibility. Compare premiums, provider networks, prescriptions, and deductibles already met. Losing job-based coverage generally creates a Marketplace enrollment opportunity; HealthCare.gov describes a 60-day window after the loss. COBRA has its own election and payment rules. Voluntarily dropping COBRA later usually does not create a new Marketplace enrollment opportunity, so confirm replacement coverage before canceling.

- **Cost and effort:** Free comparison; estimate one to two hours. Premiums and financial assistance vary by household and plan.
- **Payoff:** Reduces the risk of a coverage gap or choosing an expensive option by default.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [HealthCare.gov COBRA transitions](https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/cobra-coverage/); [Coverage options after job loss](https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/coverage/).

## 14.3 Check food, health, and energy help together

Use your state social-services agency or USAGov's benefits directory to check SNAP, Medicaid or CHIP, and energy assistance. Gather current income, household, housing-cost, and identification documents. Explain any immediate food shortage when seeking assistance. Program definitions of household and income differ, so complete each application accurately rather than assuming a single eligibility test. For an overdue utility bill, contact the utility and local LIHEAP office before a shutoff occurs.

- **Cost and effort:** Applications are free; estimate two to three hours to gather documents and begin applications.
- **Payoff:** May protect essential spending during a disruption; funding, eligibility, and processing times vary.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [USAGov benefits directory](https://www.usa.gov/benefits); [Energy-bill assistance](https://www.usa.gov/help-with-energy-bills).

## 14.4 Save separation records before reviewing a severance agreement

Keep lawful personal copies of pay stubs, benefit notices, the separation letter, and any severance offer; do not take confidential company files. Ask for final-pay and coverage dates in writing. The federal FLSA does not require severance, although agreements and other laws may matter. A release can exchange compensation for claims you would otherwise retain. Review its terms and deadlines with an employment attorney when significant rights or money are involved.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate one hour to organize records; an attorney review may cost money, with legal-aid eligibility varying.
- **Payoff:** Supports benefit applications and helps you understand the actual tradeoffs before signing.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Practice. [DOL severance pay](https://www.dol.gov/index.php/general/topic/benefits-other/severancepay); [EEOC severance waivers](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/qa-understanding-waivers-discrimination-claims-employee-severance-agreements).

## 14.5 Seek housing help while you still have time to act

If rent or a mortgage payment is becoming unmanageable, start with local assistance rather than waiting for a court date. In most US areas, 211 can connect you with social-service referrals; USAGov also links to HUD programs and local housing agencies. Explain whether you need prevention assistance, a shelter tonight, or help navigating a notice. Referral services do not guarantee a bed, funding, or legal representation, and an application does not pause court deadlines.

- **Cost and effort:** Initial referrals are generally free; estimate 30–60 minutes for first calls and expect follow-up.
- **Payoff:** Creates more time to locate available assistance and identify the next concrete step.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [USAGov emergency housing and 211 referrals](https://www.usa.gov/emergency-housing); [Legal-aid directory](https://www.usa.gov/legal-aid).
