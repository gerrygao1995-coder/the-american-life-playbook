# 01. Prevention That Fits Real Life

Start with repeatable basics and preventive care matched to your actual risks. You do not need an expensive wellness routine to take useful action.

## 01.1 Build movement into an ordinary week

Start with a walk, a wheelchair-accessible route, or another activity you can repeat. Work toward 150 minutes of moderate activity each week plus muscle-strengthening activity on two days. Short sessions count; some movement is better than none. Choose a safe starting level and adapt around disability, pain, or medical restrictions. Put the next session on your calendar before buying equipment or joining a gym.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 10 minutes to choose a routine; walking or home exercises can cost $0. Build time gradually.
- **Payoff:** Supports physical and mental health and helps preserve strength for everyday tasks.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC: Adult activity recommendations](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html).

## 01.2 Protect a realistic sleep window

Set a wake-up time you can usually keep, then allow enough time in bed for your age and needs. Most adults need at least seven hours of sleep; older adults' recommended ranges vary. Reduce late caffeine and make the room dark, quiet, and comfortable. If you regularly struggle to sleep or remain sleepy despite enough time in bed, bring a short sleep diary to a clinician.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 15 minutes to adjust your evening routine; no device or subscription required.
- **Payoff:** Adequate, refreshing sleep supports attention, mood, and health; a diary can make persistent problems easier to discuss.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC: About sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/).

## 01.3 Make a supported plan to quit smoking

Choose a quit date or a first planning appointment, identify your usual smoking triggers, and arrange support before cravings arrive. Review nicotine replacement and prescription options with a pharmacist or clinician, especially if pregnant or managing other conditions. A previous unsuccessful attempt provides information about what to change next. Use Smokefree's planning and support tools, and ask your health plan which counseling and medications it covers.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 20–30 minutes to start; counseling, medication prices, and insurance coverage vary.
- **Payoff:** A structured quit attempt gives you tools for withdrawal and reduces continued exposure to tobacco smoke.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [Smokefree: Ways to quit smoking](https://smokefree.gov/tools-tips/quit-methods).

## 01.4 Choose preventive care for your age and risks

Bring your family history, prior screening results, and vaccination record to a primary care visit. Ask which screenings and vaccines are due, which can wait, and how results would change care. Recommendations depend on age, prior results, pregnancy, exposures, and health conditions. Use current guidance rather than a fixed annual testing package. Confirm coverage before scheduling elective services; a recommended service is not automatically free in every billing situation.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 30 minutes to collect records; visit and testing costs depend on your plan and providers.
- **Payoff:** Helps you complete relevant prevention while avoiding unnecessary repeat tests and unclear follow-up.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [USPSTF: Recommended preventive services](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation-topics/uspstf-a-and-b-recommendations). [CDC: Vaccines recommended for adults](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines-adults/recommended-vaccines/).

## 01.5 Keep dental care simple and consistent

Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste and clean between teeth regularly, aiming for daily. Use an interdental tool you can handle comfortably if floss is difficult. Arrange dental checkups at an interval matched to your risk, and mention dry mouth, diabetes, or other factors that may change care. Before an expensive proposed treatment, ask what problem it addresses, how urgent it is, and whether a less costly option is appropriate.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: several minutes daily and basic supplies; professional care prices and dental benefits vary.
- **Payoff:** Supports healthy teeth and gums and helps you make informed choices about larger dental expenses.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [NIDCR: Oral hygiene](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/oral-hygiene).
