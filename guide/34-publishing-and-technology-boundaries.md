# 34. Publishing and Technology Boundaries

A small website, channel, app, or online shop can create real obligations. Check the rights to what you publish, the information you collect, and the promises you make before expanding.

## 34.1 Check reuse rights before publishing someone else's work

For each image, article, song, code component, or template, record who created it and the permission or license that covers your intended use. Follow restrictions and required notices; credit alone does not establish permission. When relying on fair use, evaluate the actual circumstances instead of a supposed safe word count or percentage. The Copyright Office says there is no universal numerical allowance. For a commercially important or uncertain use, obtain permission or qualified advice before publication.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate 15–30 minutes for a small project's rights inventory; licenses or legal advice may add cost.
- **Payoff:** Makes reuse decisions traceable and can prevent an avoidable takedown or expensive rework later.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Practice. [Copyright Office permission and fair-use questions](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html).

## 34.2 Collect less customer information and set a deletion rule

List the information flowing through forms, payment services, analytics, support messages, and backups. Remove fields you do not need and limit access to the people doing the work. Decide when information will be deleted, while checking applicable retention duties, and confirm what outside service providers retain. Review screenshots and sample files before publishing them. A privacy policy should accurately describe your practices; copying another site's policy does not make your collection lawful or your systems secure.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate one to two hours for a small service's first data inventory; implementation effort varies.
- **Payoff:** Reduces the amount of sensitive information you must protect and helps reveal unnecessary data exposure.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [FTC protecting personal information guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/protecting-personal-information-guide-business).

## 34.3 Get explicit authorization before testing someone else's systems

Read the exact vulnerability-disclosure or bug-bounty policy before security testing. Confirm authorized domains, methods, accounts, reporting channels, and stop conditions; an accessible website is not permission to probe it. Save the policy version and ask when scope is unclear. HHS, for example, limits authorization to its stated scope and tells researchers to stop when sensitive data is encountered. Another organization's policy may differ, and participating does not automatically promise payment or immunity under every applicable law.

- **Cost and effort:** Policy review is free; estimate 20–30 minutes before any testing, with clarification taking longer.
- **Payoff:** Helps keep useful research within the owner's permission and reduces accidental access to other people's data.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Practice. [HHS vulnerability-disclosure policy, an organization-specific example](https://www.hhs.gov/vulnerability-disclosure-policy/index.html).

## 34.4 Disclose paid and affiliate relationships where people will notice

When recommending something because of payment, an affiliate arrangement, employment, or a free product, disclose the relevant connection clearly with the endorsement. Use language the audience understands and make the disclosure easy to see or hear in that format. Do not bury it in a profile, a string of hashtags, or a separate page. Platform disclosure tools may help, but check the actual presentation yourself. Make truthful claims based on real experience; a disclosure does not fix a deceptive claim.

- **Cost and effort:** Usually free; estimate a few minutes per post and a review when formats or partnerships change.
- **Payoff:** Gives readers context for your recommendation and reduces avoidable advertising-compliance problems.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [FTC disclosures for social-media influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers).

## 34.5 Check accessibility and children's-data duties before launch

Test essential tasks with a keyboard, provide meaningful labels and text alternatives, and caption relevant video. Check the accessibility duties that apply to your organization; public entities and businesses can face different requirements. Separately, assess COPPA if a service is directed to children under 13 or you have actual knowledge of collecting their personal information. Age-related duties depend on the service and data practices. Review applicable laws and platform terms before enabling uploads, chat, tracking, or accounts for children.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate one to two hours for an initial review; remediation and specialist advice can require more.
- **Payoff:** Identifies barriers and obligations early, when changing the product is usually easier than after launch.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [DOJ web-accessibility guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/); [FTC COPPA questions](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions).
