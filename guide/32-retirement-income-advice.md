# 32. Turn retirement savings into a workable income plan

Accumulating an account and spending from it are different tasks. Coordinate account rules, taxes, reliable income, household obligations, and uncertainty before making choices that are difficult to reverse.

## 32.1 Track required distributions account by account

Create an inventory of retirement accounts and ask each administrator which required minimum distribution rules apply for the current year. Confirm the starting deadline using your birth date, account type, employment status, and whether you inherited the account. Do not assume one withdrawal satisfies every account's requirement. Roth accounts generally have no lifetime RMD for the original owner, but inherited accounts have separate rules. Confirm completed withdrawals before the relevant deadlines.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: one to two hours for the inventory and annual check; inherited accounts or complex tax situations may need paid professional advice.
- **Payoff:** You can identify different deadlines and distribution obligations before a missed or misdirected withdrawal creates a problem.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule / Practice. Federal rules and exceptions depend on the account and beneficiary. [IRS RMD questions and answers](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plan-and-ira-required-minimum-distributions-faqs).

## 32.2 Compare pension survivor choices before electing payments

Ask the pension administrator for estimates under each available payment form, including what a surviving spouse or other beneficiary would receive. Compare household income after either partner dies, inflation exposure, and other available resources. Review any required spousal consent and the deadline for changing your election. PBGC-managed plans generally do not permit changing the benefit form after the first payment; ask your own plan when its choice becomes final.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: one to two hours to gather estimates and discuss scenarios; a qualified adviser may charge for help evaluating a consequential election.
- **Payoff:** The household can assess survivor income alongside the initial monthly payment before making a potentially irreversible choice.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance / Rule. The linked benefit options describe PBGC-managed plans; other plan terms differ. [PBGC benefit options](https://www.pbgc.gov/workers-retirees/learn/benefit-options).

## 32.3 Ask what an annuity guarantees and what it costs

Before buying an annuity, identify the exact contract type, income start date, payout conditions, surrender charges, fees, and death benefits. Ask what happens if you need cash early and whether payments adjust for inflation. Separate contractual guarantees from illustrative returns, and evaluate the insurer's claims-paying ability. Compare the proposed contract with the income gap it would fill; putting every accessible dollar into a restrictive contract can create another problem.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: several hours to compare written contracts and obtain independent explanations; products can have explicit charges and costs embedded in their terms.
- **Payoff:** You can assess the income promise together with liquidity, risk, and the obligations that accompany it.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance / Practice. Benefits, investment exposure, and protections differ by product and state. [SEC guide to annuities](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/annuities).

## 32.4 Verify an adviser's role, background, and total compensation

Check both the individual and firm through the regulator tools linked by Investor.gov. Ask whether they will act as a fiduciary for the services you need, what that relationship covers, and how conflicts are managed. Request a dollar illustration of all fees, product expenses, and commissions. Read the applicable relationship summary, Form ADV brochure, and agreement. A reassuring title or payment label alone does not establish competence or eliminate conflicts.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: one to two hours to check records and compare written proposals; advice may be priced hourly, by project, through commissions, or as ongoing asset-based fees.
- **Payoff:** You can judge whether the service fits your decision and understand how the professional is compensated.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance / Rule. Investment advisers' duties do not erase conflicts. [SEC guide to investment advisers](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/working-investment-professional/investment-advisers).

## 32.5 Stress-test spending instead of treating a withdrawal rule as a promise

Start with essential and flexible expenses, expected income, account balances, taxes, and major future costs. Test a longer retirement, higher inflation, and poor investment returns early in the withdrawal period. Decide in advance which spending could change and how often you will review the plan. A fixed withdrawal percentage cannot guarantee that your savings will last; the right adjustments depend on your resources, obligations, and willingness to change course.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: two to four hours for an initial worksheet, with periodic updates; comprehensive modeling or advice may involve fees.
- **Payoff:** You can identify which assumptions carry the plan and which practical changes would help under an adverse scenario.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Practice / Guidance. Scenarios support planning, not a forecast of investment returns. [SEC guidance on managing lifetime income](https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/retirement-toolkit/managing-lifetime-income).
