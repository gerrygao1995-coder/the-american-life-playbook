# 12. Cars, Insurance, and Major Purchases

The expensive part of a purchase may arrive after checkout. Compare ownership costs, understand what a contract covers, and keep enough evidence to use the protections you actually have.

## 12.1 Price the whole vehicle before choosing the payment

Add the out-the-door price, financing interest, insurance, taxes, registration, fuel or charging, maintenance, parking, and likely repairs. Estimate resale value separately instead of assuming you will recover the purchase price. Compare financing offers using APR, term, amount financed, and total payments. A longer loan can make the monthly bill smaller while keeping you in debt longer. Get insurance quotes for the actual model and set a repair reserve before deciding it fits your budget.

- **Cost and effort:** Free calculation; estimate one to two hours for quotes and comparisons. Ownership cost depends on miles driven, location, vehicle condition, and financing.
- **Payoff:** Helps prevent an affordable-looking payment from crowding out the rest of your budget after purchase.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [CFPB: Calculate car and loan affordability](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-much-can-i-afford-to-borrow-for-a-car-or-auto-loan-en-751/).

## 12.2 Get an independent inspection of a used car

Arrange a pre-purchase inspection with a mechanic you choose, even if the vehicle is described as certified. Review history records, title information, and the dealer's Buyers Guide, but do not treat a history report as a mechanical inspection. Put repair promises and warranty terms in writing before paying. If a seller will not allow a reasonable inspection, consider another vehicle. Federal law does not provide a universal three-day right to return a dealer-bought car.

- **Cost and effort:** The mechanic sets the inspection fee; obtain a quote. Estimate one to two hours plus scheduling, test driving, and reviewing documents.
- **Payoff:** Can reveal repair needs before you own them and clarifies who is responsible if something fails after the sale.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Rule. [FTC: Buying a used car from a dealer](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-used-car-dealer).

## 12.3 Compare insurance using matching coverage

Use your declarations page to request quotes with the same drivers, vehicle use, liability limits, deductibles, and optional coverage. State minimums establish legal requirements, not necessarily enough protection for your situation. Ask about uninsured or underinsured motorists, collision, comprehensive, and lender requirements. Choose a deductible you could actually pay. Read exclusions and confirm coverage starts before the old policy ends. An inexpensive quote is useful only if it covers the risks you intend to transfer.

- **Cost and effort:** Comparing quotes is generally free; estimate one hour. Premiums and eligibility vary; a higher deductible shifts more claim cost back to you.
- **Payoff:** Lets you compare prices without accidentally cutting protection or creating a coverage gap.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Rule; Practice. [NAIC: Auto insurance coverage and shopping](https://content.naic.org/consumer/auto-insurance.htm).

## 12.4 Check recalls and schedule the remedy

Enter your vehicle identification number into NHTSA's recall tool before buying a used car and periodically afterward. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for any open recall, including urgent restrictions on driving or parking. Contact an authorized dealer to confirm the remedy, availability, and coverage. A clear search result is not proof the vehicle has no problems: the tool can omit very recent recalls, older campaigns, and certain manufacturers. Sign up for alerts so checking is not entirely manual.

- **Cost and effort:** The lookup and alerts are free; estimate 10 minutes. Repair scheduling can take longer; ask the dealer about the specific remedy and any associated logistics.
- **Payoff:** Connects a known safety problem to an actionable repair rather than letting a mailed notice disappear in a drawer.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [NHTSA: Check for recalls and alerts](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls).

## 12.5 Read the exit terms before accepting an add-on

Before buying a service contract or starting a subscription, identify total price, renewal date, cancellation method, exclusions, and refund rules. Check whether a service contract duplicates an existing warranty and who must authorize repairs. Save the terms and cancellation confirmation, set a reminder before a trial expires, and inspect later statements. Follow up promptly on unauthorized charges. Do not assume deleting an app ends billing or that every purchase comes with a nationwide cooling-off period.

- **Cost and effort:** Free review; estimate 10–20 minutes per contract and a monthly statement check. Cancellation charges and refunds depend on the agreement and applicable law.
- **Payoff:** Helps you avoid paying for unused services or protection that does not cover the problem you expected.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [FTC: Auto warranties and service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/auto-warranties-and-auto-service-contracts), [FTC: Free trials and recurring subscriptions](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-and-out-free-trials-auto-renewals-and-negative-option-subscriptions).
