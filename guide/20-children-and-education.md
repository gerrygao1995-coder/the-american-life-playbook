# 20 Children and education

Start with reliable care, development, nutrition, and safety. Expensive enrichment can wait until these foundations work.

## 20.1 Compare child care with assistance included

Ask each provider for its full price, hours, closure calendar, late fees, and licensing information. Then check state child care assistance and your local Head Start program before assuming the posted price is what you must pay. Eligibility, provider participation, and openings differ. Include the time and cost of backup care in your comparison, especially if missed shifts could threaten your job.

- **Cost and effort:** Free inquiries; applications and visits can take several hours.
- **Payoff:** Finds a workable care arrangement and possible help with its cost.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule + Practice. [USAGov child care assistance and Head Start](https://www.usa.gov/child-care-head-start)

## 20.2 Request a developmental evaluation when you have concerns

Write down concrete examples of the skills or behavior that concern you and discuss them with your child’s clinician. For a child under three, contact the state early intervention program directly; a doctor’s referral is not required. For preschool children three or older, contact the local public school or district special education office. Eligibility comes from evaluation and program rules, not a parent’s ability to buy private therapy.

- **Cost and effort:** Public programs offer eligible services free or at reduced cost; appointments take time.
- **Payoff:** Opens a route to appropriate support without waiting for school failure.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance + Rule. [CDC early intervention and referral routes](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/early-intervention/)

## 20.3 Check school meal help whenever household income changes

Ask your child’s school about breakfast and lunch programs, how eligibility is determined, and whether an application is needed. You can ask during the school year if circumstances change. Before summer, look for local meal sites and applicable summer grocery benefits. Availability and enrollment processes differ by program and location; do not assume a benefit continues automatically when school closes.

- **Cost and effort:** Applications are free; budget time to gather household information.
- **Payoff:** Supports regular meals and can reduce grocery pressure.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule. [USAGov school and summer meal programs](https://www.usa.gov/school-meals)

## 20.4 Assign a specific adult to watch children around water

Choose one sober adult to supervise closely without a phone, then explicitly hand off that role if they need a break. Swimming lessons and lifeguards do not remove the need for supervision. Use appropriate life jackets around natural water, restrict unsupervised access to pools, and learn CPR. Inflatable toys are not safety equipment, and water risk also includes bathtubs and unfamiliar homes.

- **Cost and effort:** Attention each time; lessons, fencing, and equipment costs vary.
- **Payoff:** Reduces preventable drowning hazards.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC drowning prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/index.html)

## 20.5 Compare 529 plans by fees and your state tax treatment

After protecting current essentials, compare your state’s 529 plan with lower-cost alternatives and read the offering documents. Check eligible expenses, withdrawal restrictions, investment risk, and any state tax incentive. A 529 is an account, not a guaranteed return. Choose investments that fit when the money will be spent and avoid committing money you may need for rent or emergency expenses.

- **Cost and effort:** Research is free; contribution amounts and ongoing fees vary.
- **Payoff:** Can make education saving more tax-efficient while keeping risks visible.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance + Practice. [SEC introduction to 529 plans](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/introduction-529-plans-investor-bulletin)
