# 29. Insure the losses you cannot comfortably absorb

Start with the people, income, property, and liabilities at risk. A useful policy pays for a defined loss under its actual terms; a familiar brand or cheap premium does not settle whether the coverage fits.

## 29.1 Match life insurance to the people who depend on you

List who would lose income or unpaid care if you died, how long they would need support, and which resources they already have. Compare term coverage for a temporary need with other options only after understanding costs and guarantees. Include a stay-at-home caregiver's replacement services. Check beneficiaries and what happens to employer coverage when employment ends; avoid choosing a benefit solely from a salary multiple.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: one to two hours to outline needs and request comparable quotes; premiums depend on underwriting, term, benefits, and insurer.
- **Payoff:** A concrete coverage target makes it easier to compare offers and identify an uncovered dependent or unnecessary feature.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance / Practice. Policy terms and state insurance rules control coverage. [NAIC life insurance guide](https://content.naic.org/consumer/life-insurance.htm).

## 29.2 Read disability coverage before your paycheck stops

Ask HR or your insurer for the disability policy and, for an applicable employer plan, its summary plan description. Write down its definition of disability, waiting period, benefit duration, exclusions, and how other benefits affect payment. Ask what you would receive after any taxes. Compare that amount and timing with essential expenses and accessible savings. Health insurance pays covered medical costs; it does not automatically replace your earnings.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: 45–90 minutes to review documents and send questions; obtaining documents generally costs nothing, while additional coverage has premiums and underwriting conditions.
- **Payoff:** You can identify an income gap while there is time to investigate coverage or adjust reserves.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance / Practice. Definitions and eligibility vary by plan. [DOL disability benefits explanation](https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/ebsa/health/67.asp).

## 29.3 Check whether liability limits match your exposure

Review the liability limits on your auto and homeowners or renters policies, then ask what an umbrella policy would add. Request its required underlying coverage, exclusions, and treatment of defense costs in writing. Consider your assets, household drivers, property use, and ability to absorb a claim. An umbrella generally addresses liability to others; it is not a substitute for coverage that repairs your own car or home.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: 30–60 minutes for an initial coverage review and quotes; the total may include higher limits on underlying policies.
- **Payoff:** You can assess a potentially large uninsured liability instead of comparing umbrella premiums in isolation.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance / Practice. Coverage depends on the underlying and umbrella contracts. [NAIC explanation of umbrella insurance](https://content.naic.org/article/whats-umbrella-policy).

## 29.4 Identify flood and earthquake gaps before a disaster

Read the exclusions in your property policy and ask the insurer to explain flood, earthquake, sewer backup, and wind coverage separately. Standard homeowners and renters policies commonly exclude flood and earthquake losses. Get quotes and effective dates for relevant separate policies or endorsements before deciding what to retain yourself. Include belongings and temporary housing in the discussion; a lender's insurance requirement is not a complete assessment of your exposure.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: one hour to review exclusions and request options; premiums, deductibles, availability, and waiting periods vary by location and policy.
- **Payoff:** You can see which plausible losses would remain your responsibility and plan around those gaps.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance / Practice. Specific exclusions and exceptions are contractual. [NAIC guide to common insurance exclusions](https://content.naic.org/article/consumer-insight-my-insurance-doesnt-cover-what).

## 29.5 Review coverage when your household changes

Set an annual reminder and revisit policies after a move, marriage, divorce, birth, major renovation, or change in household drivers. Update beneficiaries and a photo inventory of possessions. Ask whether property limits reflect rebuilding and replacement costs, and distinguish replacement-cost coverage from depreciated actual cash value. Compare the same coverage when shopping. Keep current policies until replacement coverage is confirmed effective, with any remaining gaps understood.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: one to two hours annually; an inventory can be built gradually with a phone and stored securely away from the property.
- **Payoff:** Coverage can keep pace with your household, while comparable quotes make price differences easier to interpret.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance / Practice. [NAIC policy review checklist](https://content.naic.org/article/consumer-insight-it-time-review-your-policies), [NAIC homeowners insurance guide](https://content.naic.org/consumer/homeowners-insurance.htm).
