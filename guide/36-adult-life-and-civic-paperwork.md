# 36. Starting Adult Life and Managing Civic Paperwork

Turning 18, starting work, moving, or leaving school can change who handles your paperwork. A few deliberate checks can prevent coverage gaps, missed notices, and unnecessary application fees.

## 36.1 Confirm health coverage through each life transition

Ask the plan administrator what happens when you turn 18, leave school, move, start a job, or approach 26. If a parent's plan offers dependent coverage, federal rules generally allow you to remain on it until 26, but confirm the exact end date and local provider network. Medicaid and CHIP have separate eligibility and renewal rules. Read state notices and evaluate replacement coverage early; a birthday, new address, or old insurance card does not prove continued enrollment.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate 30–60 minutes to confirm dates, costs, and options; premiums and eligibility depend on the plan and household.
- **Payoff:** Helps maintain usable coverage during a transition and exposes gaps before you need an appointment.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [HealthCare.gov coverage under 26](https://www.healthcare.gov/young-adults/children-under-26/); [Medicaid and CHIP rules](https://www.healthcare.gov/medicaid-chip/getting-medicaid-chip/).

## 36.2 Build a secure identity-document folder before an appointment

Locate your birth certificate and other required identity records, then check the issuing agency's exact application checklist. Order a certified US birth certificate through the appropriate state or territory vital-records office. For a state ID or REAL ID, use your DMV's accepted-document list rather than assuming a photo of a document will work. REAL ID is one accepted option for applicable federal identification checks; a valid passport can be another. Store originals securely and limit who receives digital copies.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate one hour to inventory documents; replacement fees, appointments, and processing times vary by agency.
- **Payoff:** Reduces repeat appointments and paid mistakes when starting work, traveling, or establishing identity.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Rule and Practice. [USAGov birth-certificate requests](https://www.usa.gov/birth-certificate); [USAGov REAL ID guidance](https://www.usa.gov/real-id).

## 36.3 Check voter registration through your official election office

If eligible to vote, use Vote.gov to reach your state or territory's registration instructions. Check your registration after moving or changing your name, and verify the deadline, accepted identification, and available voting methods well before the election. Rules differ by jurisdiction; online registration and same-day registration are not universal. Obtain polling-place and ballot-return information from your election office instead of a forwarded post. If an eligibility question applies to you, ask the office or qualified local assistance directly.

- **Cost and effort:** Registration is free; estimate 10–20 minutes for a status check and any needed update.
- **Payoff:** Gives you time to resolve a record problem and make a voting plan that fits local rules.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [Vote.gov official state links](https://vote.gov/); [USAGov voter-registration instructions](https://www.usa.gov/register-to-vote).

## 36.4 Name a medical decision-maker before an emergency

Consider who you trust to make health-care decisions if you cannot communicate, discuss your wishes with that person, and use a health-care proxy or advance-directive form valid in your state. Ask your provider or hospital where to obtain the correct form and how it must be completed. Give accessible copies to the appropriate people and update them after changes. A health-care power of attorney serves a different purpose from permission to manage your money or a will distributing property.

- **Cost and effort:** Forms may be free; estimate one hour plus a conversation, with witness, notarization, or legal-help needs varying.
- **Payoff:** Makes your preferences and chosen decision-maker easier to identify when you cannot explain them yourself.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [MedlinePlus advance-care-directive guidance](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000472.htm).

## 36.5 Complete first-job paperwork only through verified onboarding

Confirm the employer and HR contact independently before sharing identity or bank details. Read your Form W-4 instructions and keep a copy; withholding is money credited toward taxes, not a fee charged to get hired. Review your first pay stub against the agreed rate, hours, deductions, and deposit information. Reject requests to deposit an employer's check and send money elsewhere to buy equipment: fake-check job scams can leave you owing the bank even after funds initially appear available.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate 30–60 minutes for onboarding review and a few minutes to check the first payment.
- **Payoff:** Helps catch payroll mistakes early and avoids giving sensitive information or money to a fake employer.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule, Guidance, and Practice. [IRS Form W-4 information](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-4); [FTC job-scam guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams).
