# 10. Taxes and Records

Use the rules for the tax year you are filing, not the first search result you find. Federal filing tools do not automatically settle your state or local obligations. Eligibility and documentation matter more than a promised refund.

## 10.1 Check free filing options before paying for software

Start at IRS.gov's free-filing page and check the current eligibility and service scope. IRS Free File offers guided preparation through participating providers for eligible taxpayers; Free File Fillable Forms suits people comfortable preparing their own federal return. VITA or TCE may offer in-person help for qualifying returns. Confirm whether state filing is included and whether your tax situation is supported before entering everything. Book volunteer appointments early and bring the required identification and tax documents.

- **Cost and effort:** Eligible services are free; estimate 20 minutes to compare and additional time to prepare. Appointment availability, supported forms, and state charges vary.
- **Payoff:** Can eliminate preparation charges without relying on an advertised “free” product that later adds fees for your required forms.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule; Guidance. [IRS: File your taxes for free](https://www.irs.gov/file-your-taxes-for-free).

## 10.2 Recheck withholding after your life changes

Use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator after a new job, a second household income, marriage, a child, or a large income change. Gather recent pay stubs and your prior return, enter all relevant income, and read the tool's current limitations. If needed, give an updated Form W-4 to your employer and check later paychecks. Revisit the estimate when circumstances change again; a midyear adjustment may need another review at the start of the next year.

- **Cost and effort:** Free; estimate 20–40 minutes with documents available. Payroll processing takes time, so verify the change instead of assuming it happened immediately.
- **Payoff:** Brings federal withholding closer to your expected bill and helps avoid a cash-flow surprise at filing time.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Rule; Practice. [IRS: Tax Withholding Estimator](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/tax-withholding-estimator).

## 10.3 Screen for credits using your actual eligibility

Review credits relevant to your household, including earned income, qualifying children or dependents, education, and retirement contributions. Follow the IRS page to each credit's current-year requirements and check your state's tax agency separately. Keep the documents that establish income, relationship, residence, and eligible expenses where required. A refund claim needs facts, not a social-media script. Do not assume you qualify because a coworker did, or that earning too little to owe tax makes filing pointless.

- **Cost and effort:** Free to check official guidance; estimate 30–60 minutes. Complicated family, education, or business situations may justify qualified preparation help.
- **Payoff:** Helps identify legitimate benefits and reduces the chance of unsupported claims that later need repayment or correction.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule; Guidance. [IRS: Credits and deductions for individuals](https://www.irs.gov/credits-and-deductions-for-individuals).

## 10.4 Keep records according to what they prove

Create a secure folder for each tax year containing the filed return, income forms, payment confirmations, and documents supporting deductions or credits. Keep property basis and improvement records for as long as they remain relevant, including the required period after sale. Do not apply a blanket “shred after three years” rule: IRS retention periods differ for amended claims, omitted income, unfiled returns, and other situations. Check state requirements and other reasons to retain a document too.

- **Cost and effort:** Usually free using existing storage; estimate 30 minutes to organize and brief filing throughout the year. Protect access and keep a recoverable backup.
- **Payoff:** Makes a missing form, amended return, audit question, or future property sale easier to handle with evidence.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Rule; Practice. [IRS: How long to keep records](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records).

## 10.5 Verify tax demands and arrange payment directly

Check unexpected tax messages through IRS.gov and independently verified contact details. The IRS does not take gift cards as tax payment. If you owe a real balance you cannot fully pay, file on time, pay what you can, and review official payment-plan options. Eligibility, fees, and terms depend on your situation; interest and applicable penalties can continue. Handle state tax balances with the state agency separately rather than assuming an IRS arrangement covers both.

- **Cost and effort:** Official information is free; estimate 30–60 minutes to review or apply. Plan fees may apply, with some income-based relief; borrowing to pay has its own costs.
- **Payoff:** Avoids paying an impersonator and creates a documented route for managing a genuine federal tax balance.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule; Guidance. [IRS: Tax scams](https://www.irs.gov/help/tax-scams), [IRS: Payment plans and installment agreements](https://www.irs.gov/payments/payment-plans-installment-agreements).
