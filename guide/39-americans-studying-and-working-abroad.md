# 39 Americans studying and working abroad

For a semester, assignment, or longer move, verify academic, legal, financial, and medical arrangements before paying deposits.

## 39.1 Get written academic and financial approvals for study abroad

Ask your home school which credits will transfer, who awards the transcript, what aid can be used, and what happens if the program is canceled. Compare the full cost including housing, flights, local transportation, insurance, and visa fees. Review the destination’s safety guidance and keep emergency contacts. A beautiful program brochure does not establish credit transfer, scholarship eligibility, or a right to work abroad.

- **Cost and effort:** Several conversations and document checks; program costs vary.
- **Payoff:** Reduces the risk of paying for a semester that delays graduation.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Practice + Guidance. [State Department guidance for study abroad](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/safety-tips/studying.html)

## 39.2 Confirm permission to work before accepting an overseas arrangement

Check destination-government visa and work rules with the employer or sponsor before travel, including whether remote work is permitted. Get pay, insurance, housing, termination, and return-travel terms in writing. Research local laws and entry requirements; a U.S. passport is not general permission to work in another country. For a complex assignment, get immigration and tax advice that covers both countries.

- **Cost and effort:** Research is free; applications and professional advice may cost money.
- **Payoff:** Avoids a job or remote-work plan built on the wrong legal status.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance + Practice. [State Department business travel and work abroad](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/safety-tips/business.html)

## 39.3 Plan U.S. tax reporting before opening overseas accounts

U.S. citizens generally remain subject to U.S. tax rules on worldwide income while abroad. Review filing requirements, foreign tax relief, and any foreign-account reporting obligations with a qualified adviser. Keep income, tax, account, and travel-day records. Do not assume a foreign income exclusion is automatic or that owing no income tax eliminates reporting. State residency and local-country tax rules need separate attention.

- **Cost and effort:** Recordkeeping is ongoing; specialist preparation fees vary.
- **Payoff:** Helps avoid preventable reporting errors and double-tax surprises.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule + Practice. [IRS guidance for U.S. citizens abroad](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/us-citizens-and-resident-aliens-abroad)

## 39.4 Verify that your medicines are legal at every stop

Check the destination and transit countries’ rules before packing prescription or over-the-counter medicines. Ask about controlled medicines, quantity limits, documentation, and permits. Keep medicines in labeled original packaging and arrange an adequate legal supply with your clinician. Carry essential medication where you can access it during travel. A valid U.S. prescription does not guarantee that the same drug is permitted abroad.

- **Cost and effort:** Usually research and paperwork; appointments or permits may add cost.
- **Payoff:** Reduces treatment interruptions and border problems.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC traveling abroad with medicine](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/page/travel-abroad-with-medicine)

## 39.5 Keep a departure plan that does not depend on a rescue flight

Save local emergency numbers, embassy contacts, documents, and a way to reach family without your usual phone. Enroll in appropriate State Department travel alerts through STEP. Follow local instructions and use safe commercial departure options when advised and available. Government evacuation is not guaranteed and usually involves repayment; onward travel from a safe location may remain your responsibility. Include dependents and pets in planning.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning is free; emergency transport and accommodation can be expensive.
- **Payoff:** Preserves options when communications or normal travel break down.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [State Department crisis response and evacuation limits](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/help-abroad/crisis-response.html)
