# 30. Make major commitments with a full-cost plan

A bigger salary, smaller monthly payment, or exciting next step can hide costs that arrive later. Compare realistic alternatives over the period you expect to live with the decision.

## 30.1 Compare renting and buying over your likely stay

Estimate how long you could realistically stay in a home, including a job change or family move. Compare rent with ownership costs, transaction costs at both ends, repairs, and the use of your down payment elsewhere. Run scenarios with flat or falling sale prices and an earlier departure. A rent-versus-buy calculator is a way to inspect assumptions, not a prediction that ownership will make money.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: two to three hours to gather local quotes and model several scenarios; inspecting a real property and obtaining professional advice can add costs.
- **Payoff:** You can assess whether the expected stay and cash reserves support the commitment, including an inconvenient move.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance / Practice. [CFPB guide to deciding when to buy](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/prepare/consider-whether-its-the-right-time-for-you-to-buy/).

## 30.2 Compare a move using take-home pay and actual expenses

Build two monthly budgets using your present location and the proposed one. Include housing quotes, commuting, insurance, childcare, utilities, and travel to people you would still visit. Add deposits, overlap between leases, moving costs, and a job-transition reserve. Ask payroll about benefits and withholding, and check both states' revenue departments for residency and remote-work rules. A state's lack of income tax does not make every household's total costs lower.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: two to four hours initially; multistate work or unusual residency facts may justify paid tax advice before committing.
- **Payoff:** The comparison shows which costs could absorb a pay increase and how much cash the transition itself requires.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Practice / Guidance. [Consumer.gov budgeting guide](https://consumer.gov/your-money/making-budget), [IRS directory of state government tax resources](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/state-government-websites).

## 30.3 Verify an interstate mover before paying a deposit

Check the mover's registration and complaint information through FMCSA, and confirm whether you are dealing with a carrier or a broker. Request a written estimate based on a proper assessment of your belongings. Compare what is included, possible extra services, delivery terms, and liability options. Do not sign blank paperwork or rely on a phone price alone. Moves entirely within one state follow that state's oversight and rules.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: one to two hours to verify businesses and compare documents, plus time for surveys; deposits and moving charges depend on the contract.
- **Payoff:** Clear identity, scope, and pricing make it easier to spot an implausible quote before your belongings are loaded.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule / Guidance. Federal guidance here concerns interstate household moves. [FMCSA mover selection](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/select-mover), [FMCSA moving estimates](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/consumer-protection/protect-your-move/how-can-i-avoid-unexpected-moving-costs).

## 30.4 Test transportation alternatives before selling a car

Price a realistic month of transit, walking or cycling where safe, carpooling, occasional rentals, and paid rides. Include late shifts, bad weather, accessibility needs, childcare trips, and emergencies. Compare that total with the car costs you would actually eliminate. Try the alternative routine while you still have a fallback; a route that works for one commute may fail for the rest of your household's life.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: one hour to plan and a one- to two-week trial; fares, rentals, equipment, and backup rides vary locally.
- **Payoff:** A practical trial reveals whether fewer cars, fewer driving days, or a different location fits your time and budget.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Practice / Guidance. Transportation availability and safety are local. [EPA transportation alternatives](https://www.epa.gov/transportation-air-pollution-and-climate-change/what-you-can-do-reduce-pollution-vehicles-and).

## 30.5 Separate the celebration budget from the life after it

Before a wedding, a planned addition to the family, or another large commitment, price the ongoing household change alongside the upfront event. Use actual quotes and your benefits documents for childcare, leave, medical cost sharing, housing, and insurance where relevant. Agree on a spending ceiling and which costs can be postponed. Keep money for near-term commitments accessible, and revise the plan when income or timing changes.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate: a 60–90 minute household discussion, followed by collecting quotes; the planning worksheet can be free and revisited monthly.
- **Payoff:** You can see whether a one-time purchase competes with recurring needs and decide consciously where to spend or scale back.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Practice / Guidance. These are planning prompts, not standard costs for every household. [Consumer.gov guide to budgeting and saving for goals](https://consumer.gov/your-money/making-budget).
