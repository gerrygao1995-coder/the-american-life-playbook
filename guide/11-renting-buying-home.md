# 11. Renting and Buying a Home

Housing decisions shape the rest of your budget. Compare the complete living arrangement, verify local rules, and get material promises in writing before a deposit or signature makes changing course expensive.

## 11.1 Set a housing budget that survives the move

For a rental, total rent, required fees, utilities, insurance, parking, and the cost of getting to work. For ownership, add taxes, insurance, association fees, maintenance, repairs, and closing costs to the financing. Keep moving expenses and an emergency reserve separate from the down payment or deposit. Compare this total with realistic take-home income and other commitments. A lender's approval amount is not a household budget, and taxes or insurance can rise after you move in.

- **Cost and effort:** Free calculation; estimate one hour plus collecting local quotes. Use actual addresses where possible because utilities, transport, and insurance can change substantially by property.
- **Payoff:** Helps you choose a home whose ongoing costs leave room for ordinary life and unexpected repairs.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [CFPB: Decide how much to spend on housing](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/prepare/figure-out-how-much-you-want-to-spend/).

## 11.2 Read the lease and document the condition

Before signing, review total charges, renewal terms, notice periods, early termination, maintenance responsibilities, and restrictions that affect your household. Look up your state and local tenant guidance for deposits, fees, entry, repairs, and eviction procedures. At move-in, save a dated condition checklist and photos and send concerns in writing. Keep the signed lease and payment records. If a dispute escalates, seek local tenant or legal help; do not assume another state's rules apply.

- **Cost and effort:** Usually free; estimate 45–60 minutes for review and documentation. Legal review may cost money, and local aid programs may have eligibility limits.
- **Payoff:** Clarifies what you are agreeing to and creates records that can help resolve repair or deposit disputes later.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [USAGov: Tenant rights and local help](https://www.usa.gov/tenant-rights).

## 11.3 Preserve evidence of possible housing discrimination

Save listings, application criteria, messages, payment requests, and a dated account of what happened if you suspect discriminatory treatment. Federal fair-housing protections cover specified grounds, including race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, and disability. Housing type, exemptions, and state or local protections affect the details. Use the official complaint route promptly and seek legal help if housing is at immediate risk; a discrimination report does not replace responding to eviction or court deadlines.

- **Cost and effort:** Official reporting is free; estimate 30–60 minutes to organize evidence. Investigations can take time, and legal representation may require a separate arrangement.
- **Payoff:** Preserves information needed to assess your rights and lets the appropriate agency evaluate the conduct.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule; Practice. [USAGov: Housing discrimination complaints](https://www.usa.gov/housing-discrimination-complaints).

## 11.4 Compare written mortgage offers on the same assumptions

Request Loan Estimates from multiple lenders for the same loan amount, term, and loan type. Compare interest, APR, lender charges, points or credits, estimated cash to close, and whether the rate is locked. Ask about costs that can change and how long you expect to keep the loan before paying for points. Review the final Closing Disclosure against the selected offer. These forms apply to most standard mortgages; other products can have different disclosure requirements.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate several hours across applications and review. Ask about any credit-report or application charges before proceeding; closing costs remain a separate commitment.
- **Payoff:** Exposes differences hidden by a low advertised rate or monthly payment and gives you a basis for questions and negotiation.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule; Guidance; Practice. [CFPB: Review Loan Estimates](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/compare/review-loan-estimates/).

## 11.5 Price disaster coverage before committing to a home

Request insurance quotes for the exact property while you can still evaluate the purchase. Ask about rebuilding coverage, exclusions, separate deductibles, and whether flood, earthquake, wind, or wildfire risks require additional coverage or make coverage difficult to obtain. Standard homeowners coverage typically excludes flood damage. Review local hazard information even outside a designated high-risk flood zone. Include the premium and potential deductible in your budget, and ask a licensed agent to explain gaps in writing.

- **Cost and effort:** Quotes are generally free; estimate one to two hours plus follow-up. Premiums, waiting periods, availability, and deductibles depend on property, location, and policy.
- **Payoff:** Reveals a potentially unaffordable or uninsured risk before it becomes part of your housing commitment.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [CFPB: Shop for homeowners insurance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/close/shop-for-homeowners-insurance/), [CFPB: Housing costs and disaster risk](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/prepare/figure-out-how-much-you-want-to-spend/).
