# 19 Relationships and family decisions

Make the money, care, and legal arrangements visible before a stressful event forces a decision.

## 19.1 Have a money conversation before sharing major bills

Set aside a calm hour to compare take-home income, debts, recurring obligations, caregiving, and what each person wants to keep separate. Agree on how shared bills will be divided and when you will review the arrangement. Keep a record both people can access. A shared budget does not require shared passwords or surrendering control of personal money; safety matters if a partner is controlling.

- **Cost and effort:** Free; about an hour initially is an estimate.
- **Payoff:** Makes commitments and gaps visible before a lease, loan, or wedding.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Practice. [CFPB money conversation and budgeting tools](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/your-money-your-goals/toolkit/)

## 19.2 Check your state rules before signing a separation agreement

Inventory assets, debts, retirement accounts, and parenting arrangements before agreeing to a settlement. Use your state court self-help service and seek legal advice about disputed property or children. New York, for example, describes equitable distribution rather than an automatic equal split; another state may use different rules. Mediation may help willing parties, but abuse or coercion can make it inappropriate.

- **Cost and effort:** Court resources may be free; legal and filing costs vary.
- **Payoff:** Reduces the chance of surrendering rights through an uninformed agreement.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule + Practice. [New York Courts divorce FAQs — New York example only](https://www.nycourts.gov/divorce-resources/divorce-frequently-asked-questions-faqs)

## 19.3 Decide whether family money is a gift or a loan

Before transferring money, agree on the amount, purpose, repayment schedule if any, and what happens if circumstances change. Write down the agreement and give both people a copy. Consider whether you can afford never to get the money back. For a large or interest-free arrangement, ask a qualified adviser about tax and legal consequences instead of treating a text message as a complete contract.

- **Cost and effort:** Free discussion and written outline; professional review costs vary.
- **Payoff:** Reduces misunderstandings while protecting essential household spending.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance + Practice. [CFPB family lending and borrowing](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/adult-financial-education/tips-for-managing-family-lending-and-borrowing/)

## 19.4 Make a safety plan when a relationship becomes unsafe

If you face immediate danger, call 911 when you can safely do so. Otherwise, use a safer device to contact the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 800-799-7233 and plan around your circumstances. Think through a safe destination, transportation, children, medication, and important documents. Leaving can increase danger; an advocate can help plan the timing. Do not announce a departure plan to a person who may retaliate.

- **Cost and effort:** Hotline support is free; planning and access needs vary.
- **Payoff:** Connects you with help while accounting for the risk of escalation.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [HHS safety planning for an abusive relationship](https://womenshealth.gov/relationships-and-safety/domestic-violence/leaving-abusive-relationship)

## 19.5 Use the child support agency when an arrangement breaks down

Contact your state or tribal child support agency to ask about establishing parentage, obtaining an order, collecting support, or reviewing an existing amount. Keep payment records and copies of orders. If your income changes, ask about the formal modification process rather than assuming payments change automatically. Agencies can help pursue support; the government does not simply pay the other parent’s obligation for them.

- **Cost and effort:** Initial inquiry takes time; fees and eligibility depend on the program.
- **Payoff:** Provides an enforceable process and clearer documentation.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule. [USAGov child support services](https://www.usa.gov/child-support)
