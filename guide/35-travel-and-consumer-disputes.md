# 35. Travel Purchases and Consumer Disputes

Save the terms before paying and preserve deadlines when something goes wrong. The best escalation route depends on the product, payment method, seller, and applicable law.

## 35.1 Check refund rights before accepting an airline voucher

If an airline cancels or significantly changes a covered flight, save the original and revised itinerary and decide whether you still want to travel. For flights to, from, or within the United States, DOT describes refund rights when you decline qualifying alternatives or compensation. Read the current rule and the seller's instructions before accepting a voucher. Your voluntary cancellation of a nonrefundable ticket is a different situation. A refund right also does not mean every delay requires cash compensation.

- **Cost and effort:** Checking the rule is free; estimate 20–30 minutes for documentation and the first contact.
- **Payoff:** Helps you choose knowingly between replacement travel, credit, and any refund you are entitled to receive.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [DOT airline refunds](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds).

## 35.2 Verify the lodging and cancellation terms before paying

Confirm the property and booking contact independently, especially for an unfamiliar vacation rental. Save the total price, deposit terms, cancellation deadline and time zone, check-in requirements, and any separate fees before payment. Ask for a written rental agreement and use the property's or platform's verified payment process. A request to move a booking into wire transfers, gift cards, or a rushed private transaction is a reason to stop. A convincing listing or confirmation email alone does not establish ownership.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate 15 minutes before booking; verification can require a phone call using an independently found number.
- **Payoff:** Reduces preventable booking misunderstandings and exposure to rental scams, without guaranteeing the property or refund.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [FTC vacation-rental scam guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2018/07/getting-vacation-rental-watch-out-scams).

## 35.3 Preserve the credit-card billing-dispute deadline

Contact the card issuer immediately when you find an error. To preserve federal billing-error rights, ensure your written notice reaches the designated billing-inquiries address within 60 calendar days after the issuer sent the first statement showing the error. Keep copies and delivery evidence. Do not let a merchant's investigation consume that deadline. Report suspected unauthorized use promptly too. Debit-card disputes and defective-goods complaints can follow different rules; identify the issue rather than assuming every chargeback works alike.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate 30 minutes to assemble statements, receipts, correspondence, and the notice; postage may cost a few dollars.
- **Payoff:** Preserves an available dispute procedure; it does not guarantee the issuer will decide in your favor.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [CFPB credit-card billing disputes](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-dispute-a-charge-on-my-credit-card-bill-en-61/); [Regulation Z notice requirements](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/13/).

## 35.4 Read your state's small-claims procedure before filing

Use the official court website for the place where the case belongs. Check eligible claims, dollar limits, filing deadlines, proper defendant names, service requirements, fees, and any demand-letter prerequisite. Organize a dated timeline and evidence supporting the amount requested. Consider whether settlement would be practical and whether a judgment could actually be collected. Maine's court guide illustrates these procedural steps, but its rules are not national rules. Being sued also requires a timely response even if you believe the claim is wrong.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate two hours for initial preparation; court, service, travel, and collection costs vary, with possible fee waivers.
- **Payoff:** Helps you decide whether the process fits the dispute and avoid preventable filing mistakes.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Practice. [Maine Judicial Branch small-claims guide, a state example](https://www.courts.maine.gov/help/guides/sc-guide-2026.pdf).

## 35.5 Escalate with a clear record and a specific requested remedy

Build one file containing the purchase terms, receipts, photographs, dates, and messages. Contact the seller with a concise account of the problem and the remedy you want, such as a repair or refund. If unresolved, use the relevant regulator or state consumer-protection office rather than sending the same complaint everywhere. USAGov's directory helps identify the route. Keep tracking card-dispute and court deadlines separately; a complaint is not a guarantee of recovery and does not itself suspend other deadlines.

- **Cost and effort:** Most agency complaints are free; estimate 30–60 minutes to organize the record and submit it.
- **Payoff:** Makes the issue easier for a company or agency to assess and preserves evidence for further action.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [USAGov company-complaint process](https://www.usa.gov/company-product-service-complaints/).
