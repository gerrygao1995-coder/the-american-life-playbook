# 16. Freelancing and Small Business

Treat extra income as a small operation with customers, expenses, records, and obligations. A low-cost experiment can be useful; a business structure or polished website alone does not establish demand.

## 16.1 Test a specific customer problem before spending heavily

Write down one customer, one problem, one deliverable, and a proposed price. Interview likely buyers about what they already use and what it costs them. When you can legally deliver the service, try a small paid pilot with clear scope and cancellation terms. Track time, materials, platform fees, revisions, and customer acquisition effort. The goal is to learn whether someone will pay enough to support the work; compliments and follower counts are weaker signals.

- **Cost and effort:** Set an affordable loss limit; estimate several hours for interviews and price research before a pilot.
- **Payoff:** Produces information about demand and costs before inventory, debt, or subscriptions become ongoing commitments.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Practice and Guidance. [SBA market research and business planning](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/).

## 16.2 Choose a business structure for a concrete reason

Compare a sole proprietorship with an LLC using your state's filing fees, recurring costs, liability exposure, and recordkeeping requirements. An LLC is a state-law entity; federal tax treatment depends on ownership and elections, so forming one does not automatically lower taxes. Liability protection has limits, and insurance still deserves attention. Ask a qualified advisor before making tax elections or accepting personal guarantees. Keep business transactions and records separate from household spending from the beginning.

- **Cost and effort:** Initial research can be free; filing, annual fees, insurance, and professional advice vary widely.
- **Payoff:** Helps match administrative costs and protections to the business you actually operate.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Practice. [IRS LLC tax classification](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc); [SBA business structures](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/).

## 16.3 Check licenses, zoning, and tax registration before selling

Identify the state, county, city, and occupational regulators relevant to your activity. Ask whether you need a business license, professional credential, home-occupation approval, sales-tax registration, or federal permit. Online selling and working from home can still create obligations; a marketplace account or LLC filing does not answer every question. Record renewal dates and keep written guidance. Food, childcare, construction, health services, and regulated products require particular care before offering a paid pilot.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate one to three hours for initial agency checks; permit fees and approval times vary.
- **Payoff:** Identifies legal operating costs before you promise delivery or buy equipment you cannot use.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Practice. [SBA licenses, permits, and local zoning](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/).

## 16.4 Track net income and plan for taxes as you earn

Record every business payment and maintain receipts for legitimate business expenses, even when no platform sends a tax form. Reconcile records monthly and estimate profit after costs. Federal self-employment and income taxes may both apply; use current IRS instructions to determine whether estimated payments are required. If you also have a W-2 job, ask whether adjusted withholding makes sense. State taxes need a separate check. A deduction reduces taxable income; it does not make a purchase free.

- **Cost and effort:** A spreadsheet can be free; estimate 30–60 minutes monthly, with professional help for complex situations.
- **Payoff:** Makes spendable income clearer and helps reduce unexpected tax balances and avoidable penalties.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Rule and Practice. [IRS self-employed tax center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center).

## 16.5 Make honest claims and collect less customer data

Before launching an online offer, check that descriptions, prices, material restrictions, and promises match what you can deliver. Keep support for factual marketing claims and clearly disclose relevant paid relationships. Collect only information the business needs, restrict access, and choose a retention plan. Review applicable federal and state consumer and privacy rules, especially for children, health information, subscriptions, or sensitive data. A copied privacy policy cannot substitute for understanding your actual data collection and vendors.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate two hours for an initial review; regulated activities may require paid legal or technical advice.
- **Payoff:** Reduces misleading sales promises, unnecessary data exposure, and disputes that consume a small business's time.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [FTC advertising rules](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing); [FTC business security guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/start-security-guide-business).
