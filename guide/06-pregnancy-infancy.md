# 06. Pregnancy and Infancy

Prepare the medical and practical essentials before spending heavily on baby products. The most useful choices are often a care plan, suitable coverage, safe sleep, and correctly used safety equipment.

## 06.1 Arrange prenatal care and review medicines early

Before trying to conceive, or promptly after a positive pregnancy test, arrange a visit to review health conditions, medicines, vaccinations, and substance use. CDC recommends 400 micrograms of folic acid daily for people who could become pregnant; some situations need a clinician-directed plan. Ask about a suitable prenatal supplement and follow-up schedule. Do not abruptly stop a prescribed medicine because of an online warning; discuss pregnancy-specific risks and alternatives with the prescriber.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 30 minutes to book and prepare for care; appointment and supplement costs vary.
- **Payoff:** Creates an early opportunity to address preventable risks and manage existing health conditions during pregnancy.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC: Planning for pregnancy](https://www.cdc.gov/pregnancy/about/index.html).

## 06.2 Plan coverage for delivery and enroll the newborn promptly

Check coverage for prenatal visits, the delivery facility, and newborn care before the due date. Ask your plan exactly how and when to add a baby. On the federal Marketplace, birth creates a Special Enrollment Period, generally requiring action within 60 days; pregnancy alone does not, though state marketplace rules may differ. Medicaid and CHIP eligibility and postpartum coverage vary by state. Verify the baby's enrollment and effective date rather than assuming billing will arrange it.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 30–60 minutes before birth plus follow-up afterward; premiums and cost sharing depend on coverage.
- **Payoff:** Helps avoid gaps and administrative surprises when both parent and baby need care.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule; Practice. [HealthCare.gov: Pregnancy and newborn coverage](https://www.healthcare.gov/what-if-im-pregnant-or-plan-to-get-pregnant/).

## 06.3 Set up an empty, separate sleep space

Place your baby on their back for every sleep on a firm, flat, level surface in a safety-approved crib, bassinet, or play yard. Use only a fitted sheet; keep pillows, loose blankets, toys, and bumpers out. Share a room while giving the baby a separate sleep surface. Explain the same routine to every caregiver. Do not use couches, adult beds, inclined products, or a car seat as the routine sleep space.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 20 minutes to check the setup; a compliant basic sleep space is enough, with prices varying.
- **Payoff:** Reduces sleep-related hazards without requiring decorative bedding or monitoring gadgets.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [NICHD Safe to Sleep: Safe sleep environment](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/safe-sleep-environment). [NICHD Safe to Sleep: Back sleeping](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/back-sleeping).

## 06.4 Install the car seat and check its fit before the first ride

Choose a car seat that fits your child's size and your vehicle, then follow both manuals. Infants ride rear-facing, and children should remain rear-facing until reaching that seat's rear-facing height or weight limit. Install it in the back seat and use the harness correctly on every trip. Arrange a check with a qualified child-passenger-safety technician when available. Register the seat so recall notices can reach you.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 45–60 minutes to read instructions and practice installation; seat and inspection costs vary.
- **Payoff:** Helps the seat provide its intended crash protection and avoids buying a replacement simply because of a birthday.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [NHTSA: Car seats and booster seats](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/car-seats-and-booster-seats).

## 06.5 Make postpartum support and warning signs part of the plan

Before delivery, arrange help with meals, rest, appointments, and baby care. Keep postpartum follow-up appointments and tell clinicians if you were pregnant within the past year. Seek immediate medical care for warning signs such as chest pain, trouble breathing, severe headache, heavy bleeding, or thoughts of harming yourself or the baby; call 911 for immediate danger. For emotional support, the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline is 1-833-852-6262 by call or text.

- **Cost and effort:** The maternal mental health hotline is free and available 24/7. Planning estimate: 30 minutes to organize practical support.
- **Payoff:** Helps families notice urgent complications and access support during a demanding recovery period.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [CDC: Urgent maternal warning signs](https://www.cdc.gov/hearher/maternal-warning-signs/index.html). [HRSA: National Maternal Mental Health Hotline](https://mchb.hrsa.gov/programs-impact/national-maternal-mental-health-hotline).
