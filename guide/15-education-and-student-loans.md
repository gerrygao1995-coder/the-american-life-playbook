# 15. Education, Skills, and Student Loans

Buy education for a specific next step, with a realistic completion plan. Compare the cost of finishing the credential, the work it opens up, and the alternatives available locally.

## 15.1 Map a transfer path before enrolling in classes

Community college can be a practical route to a bachelor's degree, but savings depend on credits counting toward the degree you actually want. Choose a likely destination and major, then confirm transferable courses, major prerequisites, minimum grades, deadlines, and residency rules with both schools. Save the applicable agreement and an advisor-reviewed course plan. For example, UC directs California community-college students to ASSIST for course articulation; that system is a California example, not a nationwide guarantee.

- **Cost and effort:** Advising is often free; estimate two hours initially. Tuition, transportation, and transferred credits vary.
- **Payoff:** Reduces the chance of paying for credits that add semesters without advancing your intended degree.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Practice and Guidance. [UC transfer preparation and course checks](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/admission-requirements/transfer-requirements/preparing-to-transfer/basic-requirements.html).

## 15.2 Investigate paid apprenticeships before buying training

Search Apprenticeship.gov for opportunities near you, then apply directly to the employer or sponsor. Registered Apprenticeship combines paid work, structured training, wage progression, and a recognized credential. Ask about entry requirements, schedule, travel, tools, classroom costs, completion expectations, and the wage schedule in writing. Compare several occupations, including options outside construction. An available listing is not an admission offer, and a program's registration does not make every job or commute a good fit.

- **Cost and effort:** Free search; estimate two to four hours for initial research, with application and equipment costs varying.
- **Payoff:** May let you earn while developing an occupational skill and avoid some upfront tuition borrowing.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [Apprenticeship.gov career-seeker guide](https://www.apprenticeship.gov/career-seekers).

## 15.3 Compare net cost and outcomes before choosing a program

Use the Education Department's college-comparison tools and each school's net-price calculator, then compare actual aid offers when available. Include living expenses, transportation, fees, supplies, and income you would forgo. Check completion rates and program-level outcomes where available, alongside licensing eligibility for your intended occupation. Average earnings describe past students, not your future salary. Compare the likely cost to finish, especially if your schedule requires part-time enrollment or reduces paid work.

- **Cost and effort:** Free tools; estimate two to three hours for a shortlist and follow-up questions.
- **Payoff:** Makes the financial commitment clearer before a deposit, loan, or enrollment contract creates an obligation.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [Department of Education college-cost tools](https://collegecost.ed.gov/).

## 15.4 File the FAFSA and check all three deadline systems

Complete the FAFSA for each academic year through the official federal site, even if you are unsure how much aid you might receive. Check federal, state, and school deadlines separately; limited aid can have an earlier priority date. Gather required contributor information early and review the submission for follow-up requests. If your family's finances changed substantially after the tax year used, ask the school's financial-aid office about a special-circumstances review rather than changing historical figures yourself.

- **Cost and effort:** FAFSA submission is free; estimate one to two hours once documents and contributors are ready.
- **Payoff:** Opens consideration for applicable aid; submission does not guarantee a grant or a particular award.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [Federal Student Aid deadlines](https://studentaid.gov/articles/3-fafsa-deadlines/); [Financial-aid adjustment requests](https://studentaid.gov/articles/financial-aid-not-enough/).

## 15.5 Review your actual loan status before changing repayment

Log in to StudentAid.gov and your federal loan servicer to check loan types, disbursement dates, current status, payment due, and required actions. Compare currently available repayment options and total repayment estimates. As checked October 9, 2026, Federal Student Aid says SAVE has ended; affected borrowers should check their servicer's deadline to choose another plan. Eligibility is loan-specific. Do not assume a pending application stops payments or that eventual forgiveness will cover your balance.

- **Cost and effort:** Official account tools and federal repayment applications are free; estimate one hour plus servicer follow-up.
- **Payoff:** Helps avoid missed actions and align payments with options actually available to your loans.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Rule and Guidance. [Current IDR questions and SAVE notice](https://studentaid.gov/articles/faqs-idr-plan/); [Preparing for payments](https://studentaid.gov/articles/prepare-for-payments/).
