# 28. Evaluate Health Claims and Avoid Low-Value Care

A useful health decision connects credible evidence to your situation. More tests, products, and treatments can add cost and harm without improving the outcome you care about.

## 28.1 Check the evidence before buying the health claim

Before purchasing a test, supplement, app, or treatment, identify who wrote the claim, who benefits financially, when it was updated, and what evidence supports it. Look for research in people and outcomes that matter, not only testimonials or a plausible biological story. Check an independent clinical or public-health source. A confident video or AI answer can help you form questions, but it should not settle a consequential medical decision.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 10–20 minutes for a basic source check; public health information is usually free to read.
- **Payoff:** Helps you avoid paying for claims whose evidence is weaker than their advertising.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [NCCIH: Finding health information online](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/know-science/finding-and-evaluating-online-resources/finding-health-information-online/introduction). [NCCIH: Checking accuracy](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/know-science/finding-and-evaluating-online-resources/finding-health-information-online/how-do-you-know-the-information-is-accurate).

## 28.2 Buy supplements only for a clear reason

Ask what specific problem a supplement is meant to address, whether there is evidence for that use, and how you will know it is helping. Review the ingredient list and dose with a clinician or pharmacist, especially before surgery or during pregnancy. Supplements can interact with medicines, and 'natural' does not mean harmless. FDA does not approve dietary supplements for safety and effectiveness before sale; do not substitute them for necessary treatment.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 10 minutes to review each product; avoiding an unnecessary recurring purchase saves its actual price.
- **Payoff:** Reduces preventable interactions and spending on products without a clear benefit for your situation.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Rule; Practice. [FDA: Dietary supplement basics](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/fda-101-dietary-supplements). [FDA: Medicines and supplements](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/mixing-medications-and-dietary-supplements-can-endanger-your-health).

## 28.3 Question whole-body CT screening when you have no symptoms

If offered a whole-body CT scan as a general wellness purchase, ask what proven benefit it offers someone with your risks and what follow-up an unexpected finding could trigger. CT exposes you to radiation and can lead to additional tests for findings that are not dangerous. A normal result also cannot rule out every disease. Keep this separate from a scan ordered for symptoms or a guideline-supported screening program.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: one conversation before paying; include possible follow-up visits and procedures, not just the advertised scan price.
- **Payoff:** Helps avoid unnecessary radiation, worry, and expense while preserving appropriate diagnostic and screening care.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [FDA: Whole-body CT screening](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/medical-x-ray-imaging/whole-body-ct-screening-should-i-or-shouldnt-i-get-one).

## 28.4 Use antibiotics for the infection they can treat

Ask whether your illness is likely to benefit from an antibiotic; antibiotics do not treat viral illnesses such as colds or flu. Do not pressure a clinician for a prescription, borrow someone else's supply, or save leftovers for the next illness. When prescribed, take the medicine exactly as directed and ask about side effects. If symptoms worsen or fail to improve as expected, seek advice rather than changing the course yourself.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: a few questions at the visit or pharmacy; medication and follow-up costs depend on coverage.
- **Payoff:** Avoids unnecessary drug exposure and supports effective treatment when antibiotics are appropriate.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC: Antibiotic do's and don'ts](https://www.cdc.gov/antibiotic-use/about/).

## 28.5 Ask what a test or treatment would change

Before a nonurgent procedure or major treatment, ask about the expected benefit, meaningful risks, alternatives, cost, and what may happen if you wait or choose no treatment. Explain the outcome you care about most, such as pain relief, function, or avoiding side effects. Ask how and when results will reach you. For a consequential uncertain decision, seek an independent second opinion and share existing records to avoid needless repetition.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 10 minutes to prepare questions; a second opinion may have a visit charge or coverage requirements.
- **Payoff:** Helps align care with your goals and makes tradeoffs visible before you commit.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [AHRQ: Talk with your doctor](https://www.ahrq.gov/questions/resources/diagnosis/step3.html).
