# 33. Career Growth and Negotiation

Career decisions improve when you can show what the market needs, what you can do, and what an offer actually provides. Build those records before a deadline forces the decision.

## 33.1 Research the pay range for the work you actually do

Match your responsibilities to an occupation, then look up BLS wage data for your state or metro area. Compare several recent job postings with similar duties, experience, and schedules. Record each source's date and whether the number means base pay, hourly wages, or broader compensation. National medians are context, not an entitlement or a prediction. Use the resulting range to identify employers to approach and questions to ask, rather than anchoring on a single headline salary.

- **Cost and effort:** Free research; estimate one to two hours for a useful local comparison and occasional updates.
- **Payoff:** Gives your search and negotiation a more defensible starting point, without guaranteeing a raise.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [BLS wages by occupation and area](https://www.bls.gov/bls/blswage.htm).

## 33.2 Make a specific compensation request before accepting

After receiving an offer, ask about the decision deadline and which terms can change. Prepare one concise request tied to relevant experience, responsibilities, and market evidence. Rank base pay, schedule, leave, start date, relocation support, and professional development by their value to you. Use the comparison in chapter 13.5 to check the full package. Avoid inventing another offer, and obtain agreed changes in writing before accepting; some employers and pay systems have little flexibility.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate one hour to prepare and a short conversation; no paid negotiation service is necessary.
- **Payoff:** May improve an offer or reveal constraints that change whether the job fits your needs.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [University of Michigan salary and negotiation guidance](https://careercenter.umich.edu/article/salary-and-relocation-information).

## 33.3 Choose the smallest credible training step toward a real job

Pick a target role and compare its typical duties and entry requirements in the Occupational Outlook Handbook with local openings. Identify one missing skill or credential that employers actually request. Compare employer training, public workforce programs, a short course, and the apprenticeships in chapter 15.2. Ask a state workforce office about eligibility and approved funding before enrolling. A certificate is useful only if the relevant employers or licensing bodies recognize it; program availability and support vary.

- **Cost and effort:** Free initial research; estimate two hours before committing tuition or reducing work hours.
- **Payoff:** Helps direct training money toward a concrete requirement and may uncover lower-cost routes.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/); [USAGov job-training resources](https://www.usa.gov/job-training).

## 33.4 Build a small portfolio that shows your contribution

Choose two or three examples relevant to the work you want: a repair, a process improvement, a writing sample, a project, or a customer-service result. Explain the problem, your own actions, and the outcome without taking credit for someone else's work. Use materials you own or have permission to share; create a fictional example when necessary to protect clients and employers. Keep the portfolio simple, accessible, and available without a recruiter needing a special account.

- **Cost and effort:** A free document or basic website can work; estimate two to four hours for an initial version.
- **Payoff:** Makes skills easier to evaluate and gives you concrete examples for interviews and promotion discussions.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [CareerOneStop portfolios](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/JobSearch/Resumes/portfolios.aspx).

## 33.5 Keep a job-search kit ready while you are employed

Update a personal resume with accurate job dates, responsibilities, and achievements you can describe publicly. Keep credentials and personal contact information accessible outside an employer account. Ask potential references for permission and their preferred contact details, then brief them before an employer calls. Review this kit after a major project or role change. Never export confidential company records to prepare for a departure. If a layoff happens, use chapter 14 for benefit and coverage deadlines.

- **Cost and effort:** Estimate one hour initially and 15 minutes after a meaningful career change; free storage is sufficient.
- **Payoff:** Reduces administrative delay when an opportunity or job loss arrives, without making layoffs predictable.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance and Practice. [CareerOneStop work-experience guide](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/JobSearch/Resumes/ResumeGuide/work-experience.aspx); [Preparing references](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/JobSearch/Resumes/references.aspx).
