# 02. Mental Health and Substance Safety

Use support early, make care affordable where possible, and prepare for emergencies before they occur. Everyday habits can complement treatment but cannot replace it.

## 02.1 Save crisis contacts before you need them

Save 988 in your phone and share it with someone you trust. You can call or text for emotional distress, suicidal thoughts, or substance-related concerns; a crisis counselor can also help when you are worried about someone else. Call 911 for immediate physical danger, an overdose, or another medical emergency. During a difficult period, decide who you can contact and where you can go for support.

- **Cost and effort:** The 988 service is free and available around the clock. Planning estimate: five minutes to save contacts.
- **Payoff:** Makes it easier to reach a real person during a crisis without searching for a local provider first.
- **Priority:** Start here.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [988 Lifeline: Get support](https://988lifeline.org/). [911.gov: Calling 911](https://www.911.gov/calling-911/).

## 02.2 Ask for affordable treatment and an actual appointment

If cost or a long wait is blocking care, contact a nearby HRSA-supported health center and ask about mental health or substance use services, sliding fees, and referral options. If insured, verify the clinician's network status and your expected charge before booking. Ask about the earliest appointment and a cancellation list. A directory listing is only a starting point: confirm that the service is available and accepting patients.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 30–60 minutes for initial calls; health-center fees depend on income, family size, and services.
- **Payoff:** Turns a vague search for help into a concrete appointment or a clearer next referral.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Rule; Practice. [HRSA: What is a health center?](https://bphc.hrsa.gov/about-health-center-program/what-health-center). [HealthCare.gov: Getting medical care](https://www.healthcare.gov/using-marketplace-coverage/getting-medical-care/).

## 02.3 Count alcohol honestly and plan a safer change

Track what you actually drink for a week, including serving size and alcohol strength; a large beer or cocktail may contain multiple standard drinks. Use the NIAAA tools to decide whether to reduce or stop. Avoid drinking when pregnant or when medications or health conditions make it unsafe. If you drink heavily and may be physically dependent, get medical advice before stopping abruptly because withdrawal can be dangerous.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: one minute per drinking occasion to log consumption; discussing treatment may involve a visit charge.
- **Payoff:** Makes patterns visible and helps you choose a safer, more realistic change with appropriate support.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [NIAAA: Rethinking Drinking](https://rethinkingdrinking.niaaa.nih.gov/). [NIAAA: Cut down or quit?](https://rethinkingdrinking.niaaa.nih.gov/thinking-about-change/cut-down-or-quit).

## 02.4 Keep naloxone available when opioid exposure is possible

Ask a pharmacist or local health department about naloxone if you or someone close to you uses opioids or could encounter an overdose. Learn the product instructions and show others where it is kept. If someone cannot be awakened and is breathing slowly or abnormally, give naloxone if available and call 911. Follow dispatcher instructions for breathing support or CPR, and stay until help arrives even if the person improves.

- **Cost and effort:** Prices and free community programs vary. Planning estimate: 15 minutes to learn the instructions and check storage.
- **Payoff:** Can reverse an opioid overdose while emergency help is on the way; it does not replace that help.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [CDC: Five things to know about naloxone](https://www.cdc.gov/overdose-prevention/reversing-overdose/about-naloxone.html).

## 02.5 Build a small routine and notice when it is not enough

Choose one manageable daily action: a short walk, a regular meal, a relaxing activity, or a check-in with someone supportive. Schedule it rather than waiting to feel motivated. Notice whether mood, concentration, sleep, or functioning is worsening. Seek professional help for persistent or distressing symptoms, especially around two weeks or longer; you do not need to wait two weeks if symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening, or involve safety concerns.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 5–20 minutes daily; many options cost nothing and can be adapted to your circumstances.
- **Payoff:** Supports well-being and helps you recognize when additional care is needed.
- **Priority:** Build next.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [NIMH: Caring for your mental health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/caring-for-your-mental-health).
