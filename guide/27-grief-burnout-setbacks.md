# 27. Grief, Burnout, and Major Setbacks

Loss and disruption can make ordinary tasks unexpectedly difficult. Reduce the load, get specific support, and separate urgent needs from decisions that can wait.

## 27.1 Allow grief to vary and choose support that fits

After a death or major loss, expect that feelings and energy may change from day to day. You do not need to follow a fixed sequence of stages or match someone else's timetable. Choose a trusted person, a grief group, a faith community if relevant, or a counselor who can listen without pushing you to 'move on.' Tell supporters whether you want company, conversation, or help with a practical task.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: one short message to someone you trust; group and counseling costs vary, with some community support free.
- **Payoff:** Creates support that fits your needs and reduces pressure to perform recovery for other people.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [NIH Clinical Center: Loss, grief, and bereavement](https://clinical.nih.gov/introduction-loss-grief-and-bereavement).

## 27.2 Make a minimum plan for the next few days

When life is disrupted, write a short plan covering meals, medicines, sleep, essential appointments, and one person to contact. Ask for concrete help such as a grocery pickup or a ride, and pause optional commitments. Reduce repeated exposure to upsetting news if it is making functioning harder. This is a temporary way to keep basic needs covered; persistent inability to eat, sleep, or care for yourself needs professional attention.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 10–15 minutes to make the plan, then a brief daily check; practical help may be shared.
- **Payoff:** Keeps essential routines visible while leaving room for recovery and assistance.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [CDC: Managing stress](https://www.cdc.gov/mental-health/living-with/index.html).

## 27.3 Address working conditions when burnout persists

Identify what is making work unsustainable: workload, unpredictable hours, lack of control, conflict, or missing resources. Document concrete examples and ask for a specific change, such as a priority review, adjusted schedule, or clearer responsibilities. Use a trusted manager, HR, union, or employee assistance program as appropriate. Review available leave and accommodation options in [Chapter 13](13-work-pay-and-benefits.md). Personal relaxation can help, but it cannot by itself fix an unsafe or chronically unmanageable workload.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 30 minutes to document the problem and prepare a request; workplace responses and protections vary.
- **Payoff:** Focuses the discussion on changes to the job rather than adding another self-care task to an overloaded day.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [NIOSH: Work-related psychosocial hazards](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/bulletin/2024/workplace-psychosocial-hazards.html).

## 27.4 Seek assessment when distress is taking over daily life

After trauma or a major setback, contact a clinician if sleep, concentration, fear, avoidance, or substance use is worsening or making everyday responsibilities difficult. Describe what has changed and how long it has lasted; you do not need to diagnose yourself first. Treatment can help with persistent trauma-related conditions. For suicidal thoughts or crisis support, call or text 988; for immediate danger or a life-threatening emergency, call 911.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 10 minutes to write down symptoms and arrange contact; ongoing treatment costs and access vary.
- **Payoff:** Helps distinguish an expected stress response from symptoms that need additional care and support.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance. [NIMH: Coping with traumatic events](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/coping-with-traumatic-events).

## 27.5 Separate urgent obligations from irreversible decisions

After a major loss, make two lists: tasks with real near-term deadlines and choices that can wait. Get help checking bills, documents, and appointments, but give yourself time before an optional move, major purchase, or job change. If a decision cannot wait, ask a trusted person to review the options with you and write down the reason for your choice. Postponement should not mean ignoring legal, housing, or financial deadlines.

- **Cost and effort:** Planning estimate: 20 minutes for the first list and a weekly review; professional advice may have a fee.
- **Payoff:** Reduces decision overload and preserves flexibility while you handle genuinely urgent responsibilities.
- **Priority:** When relevant.
- **Basis:** Guidance; Practice. [NIH News in Health: Coping with grief](https://newsinhealth.nih.gov/2017/10/coping-grief).
