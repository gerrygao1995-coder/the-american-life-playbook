# Start here: one useful move at a time

You do not need to optimize your whole life. Use this page to choose a problem worth solving, then stop when the next action is clear. Time estimates in this guide are editorial estimates, not measured promises.

## If something is urgent

Immediate danger or serious medical symptoms: **911**. Suicide or mental-health crisis support: **call or text 988**. Possible poisoning: **1-800-222-1222**. Follow the trained responder's instructions. See [emergencies](guide/03-emergencies.md) and [mental-health support](guide/02-mental-health.md).

A court paper, eviction notice, benefit denial, shutoff notice, or insurance appeal can have a deadline. Photograph or scan it, identify the response date, and find the right help before spending hours researching the whole subject. [Start with legal help and deadlines](guide/17-legal-and-consumer-problems.md#171-find-the-right-legal-help-and-identify-the-next-deadline); [seek housing assistance early](guide/14-job-loss-and-assistance.md#145-seek-housing-help-while-you-still-have-time-to-act).

## Your 30-day reset

This is an optional sequence. Skip anything already handled; change the order for real deadlines and access needs.

### Week 1 — Protect the essentials

- [ ] Save emergency contacts and tell one trusted person where important information is kept.
- [ ] List bills due before the next two paydays. Identify any shortfall.
- [ ] Secure your primary email and financial accounts; keep a usable recovery method. [Account checklist](guide/18-accounts-identity-and-privacy.md#181-secure-important-accounts-and-preserve-a-recovery-path).
- [ ] Choose one easy health routine you can repeat. [Movement](guide/01-prevention.md#011-build-movement-into-an-ordinary-week) · [Sleep](guide/01-prevention.md#012-protect-a-realistic-sleep-window).

### Week 2 — Find one avoidable expense

- [ ] Read one statement or bill closely and request a correction if needed.
- [ ] Compare one recurring service using the total bill and exit terms. [Household services](guide/31-repairs-utilities-contracts.md).
- [ ] Check relevant help after a change in income. [Food, health, and energy assistance](guide/14-job-loss-and-assistance.md#143-check-food-health-and-energy-help-together).
- [ ] Write one question for a clinician, insurer, lender, or employer before calling. [Copy a script](TEMPLATES.md).

### Week 3 — Make the next decision clearer

- [ ] Compare one upcoming commitment using money, time, and risk separately. [Decision method](guide/40-time-decisions-and-follow-through.md#402-compare-a-purchase-in-money-time-and-risk-separately).
- [ ] Confirm the coverage, eligibility rule, or deadline that could change the answer.
- [ ] Gather a missing record: lease, benefits summary, medication list, or beneficiary information.
- [ ] Have one practical conversation about money or care. [Family decisions](guide/19-relationships-and-family.md).

### Week 4 — Keep only what helped

- [ ] Review one small experiment. Keep it, change it, or stop it. [Set a stop rule](guide/40-time-decisions-and-follow-through.md#404-set-a-review-date-and-a-stop-rule-for-experiments).
- [ ] Put a review date on any unresolved request.
- [ ] Save two relevant entries for later.
- [ ] Share one entry with someone who actually needs it, without sharing their private situation.

## Read the labels correctly

**Start here** means broadly useful protection or a foundational step. **Build next** means useful when urgent basics are covered. **When relevant** means a specific event or eligibility condition triggers it. Urgency in your circumstances overrides the label.

**Rule** identifies a policy or legal requirement; **Guidance** identifies a recommendation from a cited organization; **Practice** identifies an editorial workflow. A mixed label means the entry contains more than one kind of statement. See [methodology](METHODOLOGY.md).
